# Deep Picard regression fills a documented gap

The proposed project is a legitimate contribution and its problem class is not narrow. No paper found combines finite players, continuous time, non-nested private observation filtrations, and controls learned without computing a filter or a belief hierarchy, and four separate sources declare this exact case open or intractable in print. The nearest algorithmic precedent is Antunes, Saporito and Jaimungal (arXiv 2512.14967, first posted December 2025), which already uses **damped Picard iteration with a GRU regressed on a sub-filtration by least squares to a pathwise target, with damping weight 0.5**; it shares the inner step but not the problem, since it treats one representative agent on the common-noise filtration, solves a McKean-Vlasov fixed point, and learns the adjoint BSDE rather than the control. The nearest theoretical precedent is Wu and Jaimungal (arXiv 2412.17712), whose Theorem 4.1 is a small-horizon contraction of the same first-order-condition map for two players with nested information, and whose conclusion names "multiple informed traders with differing information sets" and "a deep learning algorithm to approximate the FBSDE system" as future work. The one exact competitor is Babichenko (arXiv 2603.12140, March 2026), a deterministic Picard iteration on filtering kernels that is confined to linear-Gaussian dynamics and to a strategy class whose generality is, by the author's own statement, the main open question. The narrowness, where it exists, is in the current evidence: scalar linear-quadratic benchmarks, two of which are themselves approximations (a series in the impact parameter, a mean-field limit, an infinite-population ε-Nash law). The additions that add most value are, in order, a non-LQ example (where no exact method of any kind exists), a small-horizon contraction theorem (Wu-Jaimungal's argument bounds conditional expectations by unconditional norms and so may not need nesting), and a scaling test to ten or more players. SIAM Journal on Financial Mathematics is the best-fitting venue, Finance and Stochastics second, Mathematical Finance third conditional on the theorem. Two citations in Babichenko's reference list are wrong, one web summary of Wu-Jaimungal is false, and one attribution in the project brief (Nadtochiy and Shkolnikov) could not be verified.

## No paper combines finite players, non-nested filtrations and learned controls

The researchers scored every candidate on five criteria: finite players, continuous time, partial information, non-nested information across players, and no explicit filter or belief hierarchy. The table below condenses their comparison. Every row was opened on arXiv or at a publisher page.

| Paper | Finite N | Continuous time | Non-nested private info | No explicit filter | What it computes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hu, deep fictitious play (1903.09376, CMS 2021) | yes, N ≤ 24 | yes | no (open-loop on joint Brownian filtration) | n/a | LQ interbank game vs closed form |
| Han-Hu (1912.01809, MSML 2020); Han-Hu-Long (2008.05519, FMF 2022) | yes, N ≤ 50 | yes | no | n/a | Markovian Nash, ε-Nash proof |
| Andersson-Andersson-Ljung (2607.08861) | yes | yes | no | n/a | geometric convergence of fictitious play, full information |
| Balkin-Ceniceros-Hu (2307.06450) | yes | yes | no (delay games, full information) | n/a | RNN per player plus fictitious play |
| Deng-Su-Zhou (2007.11781) | yes, N = 3 | yes | no (all see the same prices; priors differ) | no (Stage I RNN filter by L2 regression) | deep FBSDE after filtering |
| Antunes-Saporito-Jaimungal (2512.14967) | no (mean field) | yes | no (one common-noise filtration) | yes (elicitable regression) | MV-FBSDE with common noise |
| Guan-Shames-Summers (2608.13755, 2603.18304) | 2, zero-sum | no (discrete) | yes | no (Kalman belief hierarchy, truncated) | best response dynamics; forward-backward DP |
| Hambly-Xu-Yang (2307.15842) | 2 | not stated | yes (plus observed actions) | no (closed-form Bayesian update) | linear Nash; chain stops at second order |
| Babichenko (2603.12140) | yes, n | yes | yes | no (deterministic filter kernels) | kernel fixed point by Picard iteration, LQG only |
| Wu-Jaimungal (2412.17712) | 2 | yes | no (broker's G ⊂ trader's F) | n/a (no numerics) | small-T existence; series in impact strength |
| Cont-Xiong (Math. Finance, 10.1111/mafi.12401) | yes | yes | yes | yes (model-free MARL) | learned spreads vs known Nash and Pareto levels |

**Verified.** The deep fictitious play line is full-information by construction. Hu (2021) writes that the networks are fed "the initial states X0 and noises W[0,t] for each player's network, seeking for open-loop Nash equilibrium", and reports max relative errors of **1.15%, 2.45% and 2.95% for N = 5, 10, 24** on the Carmona-Fouque-Sun interbank game ([arXiv 1903.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09376)). The only deep FBSDE game solver found under partial information, Deng-Su-Zhou, states that "The observable filtration is the one generated by the stock prices" and that "the information heterogeneity is only on the prior estimates"; its Stage I learns the filter explicitly, "we estimate the stock returns using an L2 projection. The recurrent neural network (RNN) is used in order to exploit the time series feature of the input", before a deep BSDE stage ([arXiv 2007.11781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11781)). The closest architecture, Balkin-Ceniceros-Hu, uses "distinct recurrent neural networks to parameterize each player's controls, trained via a modified Brown's fictitious play algorithm" but for full-information delay games, and remains a preprint after three years ([arXiv 2307.06450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06450)). In control theory, Guan, Shames and Summers compute two-player zero-sum discrete-time LQG equilibria with "a noisy linear measurement of the state" per player by growing Kalman belief states, and report that "the game's value converges after only a few iterations, suggesting that higher-order belief states provide vanishing benefit" ([arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755)); their companion paper truncates at first-order beliefs by assumption and finds values "within 1%" of the unlimited-belief case with comparable noise and "bounded within 10%" when noise is very asymmetric ([arXiv 2603.18304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18304)). The multi-agent RL finance papers learn on observation histories but compare to the full-information Nash equilibrium and report drift toward collusion; none targets the partial-information equilibrium as the object to compute ([Cont-Xiong, Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:38c0a628-d6cc-4160-be23-9337a1c02951); [Lillo-Macrì, arXiv 2408.11773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11773)).

**Verified.** Babichenko is the only paper claiming an exact solution of the target class. His abstract reads: "We provide the first exact equilibrium characterization of finite-player continuous-time linear-quadratic games with endogenous signals. Estimating primitive shocks rather than the state collapses the belief hierarchy onto deterministic impulse-response maps, reducing Nash equilibrium to a deterministic fixed point with no truncation and no large-population limit." His two-player example is computed on "a uniform grid of N = 40 points; no Monte Carlo simulation is needed", with "The Picard residual reaches 10^−5 in 19 iterations (≈ 10 ms in C++)". Uniqueness holds only for short horizons (Theorem D.5), existence for all horizons without uniqueness (Theorem D.6), and "The main open question is whether the deterministic impulse-response class is without loss of generality" ([arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)).

**Inference.** The proposed method occupies an empty cell. Its distinguishing move, regressing the first-order-condition target directly on each player's own observation increments, appears nowhere in the deep-game literature; Deng-Su-Zhou perform the same statistical operation but on the drift, and then solve a full-information problem. Babichenko's Remark 4.1 ("each observation increment is affine in the noise increments, so by joint Gaussianity the conditional mean is a deterministic linear projection") is the theoretical reason the regression is exact in the LQG case, which makes his kernel method the correct exact benchmark in that case and the method's boundary outside it. The Guan-Shames-Summers numbers, 1% to 10% value gap from belief truncation, are a baseline a hierarchy-free method should beat or match.

## Antunes, Saporito and Jaimungal share the inner step, not the problem

**Verified.** The paper "Deep Learning and Elicitability for McKean-Vlasov FBSDEs With Common Noise" (v1 16 Dec 2025, v3 10 Oct 2026, 28 pages, no journal reference) solves MV-FBSDEs "combining Picard iterations, elicitability and deep learning" ([arXiv 2512.14967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14967)). Its structure matches the proposed method step for step. The conditional expectation is a regression on a sub-filtration: "we solve the minimization problem (3) using X^{k+1}_t. We approximate s ∈ L^2[F^0_t] as a recurrent neural network s(t, (W^0_s)_{s≤t})", with the quadratic case "S^{k+1}_t = argmin_{s ∈ L^2[F^0_t]} E[(ϕ(X^{k+1}_t) − s)^2]". The backward process is also a least-squares projection of a pathwise target: "Y^{k+1}_t = argmin ... E[(Y − (G(X^{k+1}_T, S^{k+1}_T) + ∫_t^T f(u, X^{k+1}_u, Y^k_u, ...) du))^2]". The outer loop is damped: "we dampen the full iteration by performing 'soft-updates' of the processes as follows Ψ^{k+1} = δ Ψ^k + (1 − δ) Ψ̂^{k+1}", with **δ = 0.5 "following the fictitious play methodology"**. The network is a GRU on the conditioning path with "18 units and the SiLU activation"; training uses "K = 20 (outer) Picard iterations" of "2,000 backpropagation iterations" on batches of 8,192 paths, about 8 minutes per outer iteration on a T4. Version 3 adds a one-step contraction estimate of the form "E^k_i ≤ ρ(h) E^{k−1}_i + Γ δ^k_i + Γ^2 h τ^{k−1}_i + κ(h)" with ρ(h) < 1 for small step h. Code is public ([alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2512.14967)).

**Verified.** The problem is different on every structural axis. The sub-filtration is the single common-noise filtration F^0 of a representative agent, "S_t = E[X_t | F^0_t]"; the fixed point is over the measure flow; the unknowns are "the backward process and the conditional expectations arising from common noise", with Y parameterized as "U(t, X_t, (W^0_s)_{s≤t})" by a hybrid feedforward and GRU decoupling field; and the examples are the Carmona-Fouque-Sun systemic-risk model (plus a quantile variant) and an Aiyagari-Bewley-Huggett growth model. The conclusion mentions neither finite players nor heterogeneous information. The paper names its own closest competitor as Hu, Jin, Laurière and Zhang, who "integrate signature representations with Deep BSDE solvers inside a fictitious-play loop" for common-noise MFGs ([arXiv 2511.09112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09112)).

| Axis | Antunes-Saporito-Jaimungal | Proposed method | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outer loop | damped Picard, δ = 0.5, soft update | damped Picard, iterate averaging | full |
| Regression target | pathwise target, L2 (quadratic score) | pathwise Λ^i, L2 | full |
| Regressor | GRU on common-noise path W^0 | GRU on player i's observation increments | architectural |
| Conditioning σ-field | one F^0, sub-filtration of the full filtration | N filtrations F^i, none nested in another | none |
| Object learned | adjoint (Y, Z, Z^0) via decoupling field, plus S_t | control u^i from explicit first-order condition | none |
| Fixed point | McKean-Vlasov measure flow | N best responses | none |
| Players | one representative agent | finite N | none |
| Theory | one-step contraction (v3) | none yet | gap on the proposed side |
| Examples | CFS systemic risk, Aiyagari growth | broker-trader, mean-field broker, major-minor, two-trader | none |

**Inference.** A referee who knows 2512.14967 will call it the nearest prior art, and the honest description is that the inner computational step is the same and the problem, the object computed, and the information structure are different. The paper does not subsume the proposed method; nothing in it is non-nested, and the mean-field fixed point removes exactly the bilateral channel (my action moves your signal) that makes the finite-N case hard. Conversely the proposed method does not subsume theirs: it uses only the quadratic score, while elicitability covers quantiles and expected shortfall. The paper's v3 error bound shows the group is now writing convergence theory for damped Picard plus regression, and the group's own future-work statements (next section) point at the finite-N multi-filtration case. The timeliness risk is real, and the positioning should be written now, not after submission.

## Four devices close belief hierarchies; finite-N endogenous signals stay open

### Macroeconomics: exogenous signals collapse, endogenous signals do not

**Verified.** Townsend's problem is defined by Chahrour and Jurado as a situation in which "the action of each agent depends not only on its own forecast of the exogenous state of the economy, but also on its forecast of the average forecasts of others, and on its forecast of the average forecast of that average forecast, and so on, ad infinitum" ([NBER WP 33794](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)). Huo and Takayama prove that "the equilibrium outcome always admits a finite-state representation when the signals follow finite ARMA processes", and state the limit of the result: "This result holds when signals follow ARMA processes, a condition that can be guaranteed when the information flow is exogenously determined" ([RESTUD 92(5), 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/restud/v92y2025i5p3138-3173..html); [2021 WP](https://zhenhuo.weebly.com/uploads/2/6/4/8/26480716/paper_ht_2021.pdf)). With endogenous signals, "the aggregate outcome in equilibrium never admits a finite-state representation" in their Example 1, and "very often there exist multiple equilibria". Chahrour and Jurado's Proposition 10 proves that in Townsend's noisy-price economy "There does not exist a symmetric REE in which {y_it, k_it, p_it} have finite-order ARMA representations", and they state that the earlier closed forms of Sargent (1991) and Kasa (2000) are "incorrect". The available methods are four: modify the information structure so shocks become common knowledge after finitely many periods (Townsend's own device), truncate the belief order (Nimark 2017), approximate by ARMA processes, or iterate in the frequency domain ([NBER WP 33794](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)).

**Inference.** A trading game in which the price carries the other trader's order flow is an endogenous-signal problem in exactly this sense, so the macro results predict no finite-dimensional filter state. The macro methods all presume stationarity and an infinite past and do not transfer to a finite-horizon execution game. The dividing line is endogenous versus exogenous signals, not the presence of higher-order beliefs.

### Kyle models: symmetry and the market maker's pricing rule

**Verified.** Dynamic Kyle models with several insiders are solved only under a static symmetric signal structure. Choi and Larsen (July 2026) write: "To reduce the model's complexity, Foster and Viswanathan (1996) place a symmetry assumption on the traders' information structure. The symmetry assumption produces a one dimensional second-order recursion", and in a footnote, "Foster and Viswanathan (1994) give an example of a Kyle model with an asymmetric information structure which leads to a two dimensional Kalman filter. Such extensions are not covered by our analysis." Existence for Foster-Viswanathan was open until this paper: "no existence proof is available to guarantee that the model in Foster and Viswanathan (1996) is supported by a Nash equilibrium" ([arXiv 2607.15057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15057)). Back, Cao and Willard give the continuous-time version with "the unique linear equilibrium" for imperfectly correlated signals ([J. Finance 55(5), 2000](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v55y2000i5p2117-2155.html)). Lambert, Ostrovsky and Panov allow "essentially arbitrary correlations" but in a single period ([Econometrica 86(4), 2018](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20516)). Choi, Larsen and Seppi note that "the insider's, market makers', and rebalancer's information sets are not nested" and solve the discrete-time case numerically per period, listing continuous time and "multiple insiders and rebalancers" as extensions ([arXiv 1502.02083](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02083)). Babichenko summarises: these models "close the belief hierarchy through model-specific conjectures" and his Section 5 targets "a setting where guess-and-verify breaks down once traders differ in precision or learn continuously from private sources" ([arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)).

**Inference.** Three devices act together in every solved multi-insider Kyle model: the competitive pricing rule fixes the public belief, signal symmetry reduces each insider's belief about the others to a scalar statistic, and a linear ansatz with a quadratic value function replaces the hierarchy. The open cases, by the sources' own words, are asymmetric signal structures, continuously arriving private signals for several insiders, and continuous-time insider-plus-rebalancer models. An Almgren-Chriss impact price removes the market maker and with it the first device, which is one reason the hierarchy does not close by the Kyle route.

### Continuous-time trading games: mean-field limits, nesting, observed actions, zero-sum

**Verified.** Every solved continuous-time trading game with differing filtrations uses one of four devices. Casgrain and Jaimungal (2018) reduce to a common filter because "the projection of the dynamics of S onto the filtration F will be identical to the projection onto F^j", and in the mean-field limit "each agent's individual contribution to ν^(N) vanishes" ([arXiv 1803.04094](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04094)). Their 2020 paper states the finite-player obstacle directly: "Obtaining this collection of strategies for the stochastic game with a finite number of players proves to be a difficult task ... One of the main obstacles in finding a solution to this problem is that each agent's strategy is adapted to different filtration F^j" ([arXiv 1810.06101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06101), Math. Finance 30(3), 2020). Bergault and Sánchez-Betancourt, whose finite-player model has exactly N private OU signals plus a common signal plus a common impacted price, write: "This system of N + 1 HJB equations is intractable in practice for a large number of informed traders. To obtain an approximate solution to this problem, in the remaining of the paper we propose a mean field game approach" ([arXiv 2401.05257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05257), SIFIN 16(2), 2025). Wu and Jaimungal solve the two-player case where the broker's filtration G is nested in the trader's F ("H^2_G is a closed subspace of H^2_F"), prove small-T existence by a Banach contraction of the map "Φ_{t,1}(Υ) = (1/2) a^{-1} E[... | G_t] ... Φ_{t,2}(Υ) = (1/2) b^{-1} E[... | F_t]", and conclude: "while we cannot solve the FBSDE system in closed-form, we are able to approximate the solution to arbitrary order in the impact of trading" ([arXiv 2412.17712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17712)). Donnelly and Li close the hierarchy by invertibility: "when broker j supplies liquidity to the informed trader, she is able to deduce the total trading speed of the informed trader", after which the alpha is recovered exactly ([arXiv 2503.08287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08287)). Cardaliaguet and Rainer treat "a two-player zero-sum stochastic differential game in which the players have an asymmetric information on the random payoff" by dual viscosity solutions, with a type drawn once ([AMO 59(1), 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00245-008-9042-0)). Evangelista and Thamsten write the general finite-N FBSDE with projections, "the average ... is not necessarily F^i-adapted, whence we assume that player i projects it", prove uniqueness under a weak-interaction condition, and compute no stochastic private-signal example ([arXiv 2004.00790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00790)). Firoozi and Caines state the control-theory version: "the problem of partially observed MM LQG MFG systems in the finite population case is an open problem. This is because in order to compute its best response strategy ... an agent requires estimates of other agents' states, and for that they need to estimate other agents' strategies; this leads to the generation of second-order and higher-order estimates. Hence each agent will have an infinite-dimensional extended system" ([arXiv 1810.04369](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)). Team theory supplies the structural reason: Ho and Chu's Theorem 2 gives linear optimality only for partially nested structures, where "if a DM's action affects our information, then knowing what he knows will yield linear optimal solutions", and Witsenhausen's two-stage counterexample shows nonlinear strategies strictly dominate once "the first action affects the second observation without making the first controller's information available to the second" ([Ho-Chu 1972](https://mcrotk.github.io/courses/references/ho_chu.pdf); [Witsenhausen 1968](http://www.control.ece.ntua.gr/UndergraduateCourses/StochElegx/H.%20S.%20WITSENHAUSEN.pdf)).

**Inference.** The two-trader game with private OU alphas and a common price with permanent impact from both is not solved anywhere. Each nearest paper removes one ingredient: Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt have the signals but only the broker impacts and only the mean-field limit is solved; Wu-Jaimungal have two players and one price but nested information and a series, not a closed form; Evangelista-Thamsten have finite N and impact from all but no computed projections; Babichenko has finite n and endogenous private signals but a market-maker price, the LQG restriction, and no numerical results for his trading embedding. In the game, trader 1's orders enter the price trader 2 observes without trader 2 seeing trader 1's alpha, so by Ho-Chu's definition the structure is not partially nested and linear optimality is not guaranteed. The claim "no closed form exists" rests on the absence of one in the sources plus the explicit intractability statements of Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt, Casgrain-Jaimungal and Firoozi-Caines, not on a theorem. Within LQG, Babichenko's kernel method is the only exact approach that applies in principle and should be the comparison; outside LQG (Markov-chain alphas, nonlinear impact, non-quadratic penalties) no exact method of any kind was found, and the mean-field limit is the only solved route.

## Three communities have asked for this solver in print

**Verified.** The most direct call is Wu and Jaimungal's conclusion: "It would also be interesting to investigate the setting with multiple informed traders with differing information sets. Finally, for practical purposes, it would be useful to develop a deep learning algorithm to approximate the FBSDE system" ([arXiv 2412.17712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17712)). A cancelled GERAD seminar abstract from May 2025 said the speaker would "discuss a new deep learning approach for approximating the solution to the system of FBSDEs" if time permitted, and no corresponding preprint was found by the researchers as of 18 November 2026 ([GERAD event 2332](https://www.gerad.ca/en/events/2332)). Macrì, Jaimungal and Lillo, after building a single-agent GRU that "acts as a model-free approximation of the filtering map", close with "A natural extension is to study how such information affects learning, interaction, and equilibrium behaviour in multi-agent settings, particularly in the presence of price impact and heterogeneous information" ([arXiv 2511.00190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00190)). Aqsha, Drissi and Sánchez-Betancourt call their paper "together with Wu and Jaimungal (2024) ... among the first to study settings in which both liquidity providers and takers optimise their strategies while filtering missing information", assume parametric OU forms for each side's unobserved process, and leave an "equilibrium of beliefs" as "an interesting avenue for future research" ([arXiv 2412.20847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20847)). Ralston, Yang and Hu substitute a heuristic because the equilibrium is not computable: "we define implementable controls by averaging the full-information Nash equilibrium under each player's prior", with "incorporating dynamic learning and belief updating" as future work ([arXiv 2603.17197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17197)). In control, Guan, Shames and Summers write "there are no existing algorithms to jointly solve these coupled equations to the best of our knowledge" and "for general games lacking this common information structure, to the best of our knowledge, no known sequential decomposition method exists" ([arXiv 2603.18304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18304); [arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755)). Li, Hammar, Stadler and Zhu state that "current methods are limited to particular information structures to avoid belief hierarchies" and that the shared-belief assumption "is not realistic for most practical scenarios" ([arXiv 2402.18781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18781)). Hambly, Xu and Yang record that "stochastic games with private beliefs have been identified as an open problem in the past decade" ([arXiv 2307.15842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15842)).

**Inference.** The first users are the broker-trader groups in Toronto, Oxford and Paris, whose benchmark papers appeared in Finance and Stochastics (2026) and SIFIN (2025) and who have an unsolved two-filtration FBSDE waiting; a solver that takes their model and returns the equilibrium for N informed traders with private signals, which Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt declare intractable, is directly usable. The second group is the control community working on LQG games with asymmetric information, who have stated the absence of algorithms most sharply but who will expect a comparison to belief-truncation and common-information baselines. The third is the multi-agent RL finance literature (Cont-Xiong, Lillo-Macrì, Koulouris-Campajola), which currently measures learned behaviour against the full-information Nash equilibrium because no partial-information benchmark exists. Kyle theorists (Choi-Larsen) and macroeconomists (Chahrour-Jurado) face the same hierarchy but in stationary or market-maker settings, so they are an audience for the idea rather than users of the code. No finance paper states "a numerical method for non-nested filtrations is needed" in those words; the finance demand is implicit in characterisations without solutions, and the explicit statements are in control.

## Verdict: legitimate, broad in problem class, narrow in current evidence

**Inference.** The contribution is real and not narrow. Its problem class, finite-player Nash equilibria with non-nested private filtrations, is declared open or intractable by four independent sources across finance, control and economics, and no existing numerical method addresses it in continuous time. What is narrow at present is the evidence: scalar LQ examples, benchmarks that are not exact finite-N solutions, and no theory. The strongest framing is the one the sources themselves supply: the method solves the fixed-point map that Wu-Jaimungal prove contractive for two nested players, in the finite-N non-nested case that Wu-Jaimungal, Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt and Firoozi-Caines leave open, by regressing each player's first-order-condition target on its own observation history, so that no filter, no belief hierarchy and no adjoint BSDE is ever formed. The second framing, that it is a learned Monte Carlo version of Babichenko's deterministic Picard iteration that drops the LQG and noise-state-linear restrictions, is accurate and should be stated, with one caution: in a Gaussian LQ game started from linear iterates, the Picard iteration stays in the linear class (the conditional expectation of a linear functional of Gaussians on Gaussian observations is linear), so the method tests Babichenko's open question only if started from nonlinear iterates or applied outside LQG. The claim should be "does not assume the class", not "shows the class is restrictive".

**Inference.** A caveat about the benchmarks belongs in the paper. None of the three is an exact finite-N non-nested equilibrium. Wu-Jaimungal supply a series in the impact strength h with closed-form coefficients, exact only as the series is summed; Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt supply the mean-field limit, in which "the individual signal of a trader seems to have little impact on his trading strategy"; Firoozi-Caines supply infinite-population control laws with an ε-Nash guarantee, applied in their own simulation to 101 agents. Agreement with these is agreement with an approximation, and the discrepancy at finite N is partly real. The new two-trader game has no benchmark at all, so its validation must come from internal checks (residual of the first-order condition, stability across seeds, convergence in sample size and network size, and in the LQG case agreement with a Babichenko-style kernel iteration or a long-horizon Guan-style belief-truncation solver).

The additions rank as follows.

**Inference.** The non-LQ example is ranked first because it is the only addition that changes the nature of the claim: with it, the method does something no exact approach can do; without it, a referee can say Babichenko's kernel fixed point already computes the same LQG objects deterministically in milliseconds. The theorem is ranked second because it determines which venues are open rather than what the contribution is. More players ranks above dimension because the open problem is in N and in filtrations, not in state dimension.

**Verified.** On venues, SIFIN's scope says it "publishes articles introducing new methods and algorithms representing significant (as opposed to incremental) improvements on the existing state of affairs of modern numerical implementations of applied financial mathematics" and accepts Short Communications of at most 10 pages ([LetPub scope page](https://www.letpub.com/index.php?journalid=9638&page=journalapp&view=detail)); it published Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt. **Inference.** The de facto referee standard, read off the accepted deep-game papers, is a convergence or ε-Nash result, an LQ benchmark with explicit solution, one example without closed form, and a scalability test. The project currently has the second and, in the two-trader game, the third. A SIFIN submission would likely be asked for the convergence statement, an error decomposition, a test beyond scalar games, and one comparison method. Antunes et al. report mean errors of order 10^-2 on the CFS model at d = 1 and about 4 to 7 × 10^-2 at d = 10; reviewers will ask whether the proposed method's errors fall with network size, sample size and iteration count.

## Conclusion

The literature has spent four decades closing belief hierarchies by removing the bilateral channel: average it away in a mean-field limit, assume one player knows everything the other knows, let actions reveal signals, or restrict to zero-sum games with a type drawn once. The proposed method is the first found that keeps the channel and does not represent the hierarchy at all, because the fixed point it iterates is on controls, not beliefs, and each control is a regression on observed data that the player actually holds. This reframes the open problem: the obstacle in Firoozi-Caines and Guan-Shames-Summers is representing the infinite-dimensional belief state, and a method that never represents it is not an approximation of the hierarchy but a change of unknowns. Babichenko reached the same conclusion by analytic means for LQG, and the two approaches are complementary evidence that the regress is an artefact of the state-space representation rather than of the game.

The practical consequence is that the paper's weight should shift from the three LQ benchmarks, which confirm correctness against approximations, to the one example where nothing else works. A Markov-chain latent alpha in a two-trader impact game, solved by the same code, with a short-horizon contraction stated in the Wu-Jaimungal style, would make the submission difficult to dismiss at SIFIN or Finance and Stochastics and would pre-empt the follow-up that the Toronto group has twice announced as future work.
