# Belief hierarchies in continuous-time games with private information: literature notes

Scope: how the infinite regress of beliefs ("forecasting the forecasts of others") is closed in finance and economics models with several privately informed agents, which cases are known to be intractable, and whether a finite-player two-trader game with private OU alpha signals and a common price with permanent impact from both traders has been solved. Date of search: 18 November 2026.

Verification convention. Every bibliographic entry below was checked by opening at least one of: the arXiv full text (via alphaXiv), the publisher or RePEc record, or a verified citing paper. Items marked "not opened" were seen only in a citing paper or a search snippet. Two citation errors in the literature are flagged in Q1 and Q4.

## Q1. Townsend (1983) and the state of its resolution (Kasa 2000, Nimark 2017, Huo and Takayama, Angeletos and Lian, Chahrour and Jurado 2025)

### Takeaway
Townsend's problem is that when agents learn from endogenous aggregates, each agent's forecast depends on the average forecast of others, and so on without end. For exogenous (policy-invariant) ARMA signals the hierarchy collapses to a finite state (Huo and Takayama, RESTUD 2025). For endogenous signals (signals that depend on the equilibrium actions of others), Chahrour and Jurado (NBER 2025) prove that Townsend's own noisy-price model has no finite-dimensional state, that the earlier closed-form claims of Sargent (1991) and Kasa (2000) are incorrect, and that the only available methods are approximations (information truncation, belief-order truncation, ARMA approximation, or frequency-domain iteration).

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic record: Robert M. Townsend, "Forecasting the Forecasts of Others", Journal of Political Economy 91(4), 546-588, August 1983, DOI 10.1086/261166. RePEc lists "No abstract is available for this item." I could not open the full text (JSTOR and journals.uchicago.edu return 403). — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jpolec/v91y1983i4p546-88.html)
- Definition of the problem (Chahrour and Jurado 2025, p. 20): "the 'infinite regress problem' refers to a situation in which the action of each agent depends not only on its own forecast of the exogenous state of the economy, but also on its forecast of the average forecasts of others, and on its forecast of the average forecast of that average forecast, and so on, ad infinitum." — [Chahrour and Jurado, NBER WP 33794, May 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- Why it is a technical problem (same source, p. 20): "it may cause the equilibrium dynamics to fail to have a finite-dimensional state-space representation, making it infeasible to use standard Kalman filtering formulas to compute agents' expectations." — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- Townsend's own device: Townsend (Sec. 8) assumed firms observe the average price with noise, and made the model computable by "modify[ing] the information structure of agents in the model by assuming that all exogenous disturbances become common knowledge after a finitely many periods. This is the approach taken by Townsend (1983), originally proposed by Chari (1979)." — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025, p. 23](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- Impossibility result (Proposition 10): "Consider the economy from Sec. 2, with n to infinity and s_it = (u_it, p-tilde_t) ... There does not exist a symmetric REE in which {y_it, k_it, p_it} have finite-order ARMA representations." The authors add: "This formally confirms Townsend's original conjecture that the infinite regress of higher-order beliefs in this model leads to an infinite state problem, despite evidence to the contrary from the existing literature." — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025, pp. 1, 22](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- Earlier closed forms are wrong (same source, p. 22): "Sargent (1991) claims that the inclusion of 'moving average components in agents' perceptions and of lagged innovations ...' enables [him] to formulate the equilibrium as a fixed point of a finite-dimensional operator ... However, only numerical evidence is provided to support this claim, and it turns out to be incorrect (as Proposition 10 will show). Kasa (2000) presents the closed-form solution to the model, in Proposition 2.2.3, and it has a finite-dimensional state-space representation. However, the closed-form solution presented there is incorrect." — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- The full-revelation case is different: when firms observe an appropriately weighted price index without noise, "the infinite regress problem does not lead to an infinite state problem" and the REE coincides with the full-communication equilibrium (Propositions 2, 6, 8, 9). Pearlman and Sargent (2005) summarised the earlier literature as saying higher-order beliefs "disappear from this environment" because prices reveal everything; Chahrour and Jurado show this summary is misleading because Townsend's Sec. 8 economy observes prices with error. — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025, pp. 20-21](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- Available numerical methods (same source, p. 23): three in the literature, "The first is to modify the information structure ... The second is to include only a finite number of higher-order expectations, as in Nimark (2017). The third is to use ARMA processes to numerically approximate the equilibrium dynamics ... Sargent (1991), ... Han et al. (2022), Adams (2022), and Huo and Takayama (2023)." Chahrour and Jurado add a fourth: iterate the fixed point in the frequency domain with the Wiener-Kolmogorov filter. — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)
- Kasa (2000) bibliographic record: Kenneth Kasa, "Forecasting the Forecasts of Others in the Frequency Domain", Review of Economic Dynamics 3(4), 726-756, October 2000, DOI 10.1006/redy.1999.0085. - Nimark (2017): Kristoffer Nimark, "Dynamic Higher Order Expectations", CEPR Discussion Paper 11863 (Feb 2017); SED 2017 Meeting Paper 1132; SSRN 2924683. Abstract: proves a unique equilibrium exists in linear dynamic rational-expectations models with privately informed agents, assuming agents discount the future and exogenous processes follow "stationary (but otherwise unrestricted) VARMA processes"; "The algorithm is a contraction mapping" that recursively computes increasing orders of expectations; "the equilibrium dynamics of the model can be approximated to an arbitrary accuracy with a finite-dimensional state" with "explicit bounds on the approximation errors". I found no journal publication record; Babichenko cites it as a 2017 Cornell working paper. — [IDEAS/RePEc CEPR DP](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cpr/ceprdp/11863.html); [SED listing](https://ideas.repec.org/p/red/sed017/1132.html)
- Huo and Takayama: Zhen Huo and Naoki Takayama, "Rational Expectations Models with Higher-Order Beliefs", Review of Economic Studies 92(5), 3138-3173, October 2025, DOI 10.1093/restud/rdae096. Main result (2021 WP abstract): "we prove that the equilibrium outcome always admits a finite-state representation when the signals follow finite ARMA processes." — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/restud/v92y2025i5p3138-3173..html); [2021 working paper](https://zhenhuo.weebly.com/uploads/2/6/4/8/26480716/paper_ht_2021.pdf)
- Huo and Takayama on the scope of the finite-state result (2021 WP, p. 2): "This result holds when signals follow ARMA processes, a condition that can be guaranteed when the information flow is exogenously determined. The key observation is that recording all higher-order expectations indeed requires an infinite number of state variables, but the equilibrium outcome only depends on a weighted average of them which surprisingly collapses to a low dimensional object." — [Huo and Takayama 2021 WP](https://zhenhuo.weebly.com/uploads/2/6/4/8/26480716/paper_ht_2021.pdf)
- Huo and Takayama on endogenous signals (Section 5, Proposition 5.2): in Example 1 (an exogenous signal plus a noisy signal of the aggregate action), "the aggregate outcome in equilibrium never admits a finite-state representation"; in Example 2 (perfect observation of the lagged aggregate), it fails "when alpha is sufficiently large". And: "the infinite-state result is not due to higher-order expectations per se ... With endogenous information, the additional complication lies in that the signal process itself cannot be represented as a finite-order process." — [Huo and Takayama 2021 WP, pp. 29-30](https://zhenhuo.weebly.com/uploads/2/6/4/8/26480716/paper_ht_2021.pdf)
- Angeletos and Lian: George-Marios Angeletos and Chen Lian, "Incomplete Information in Macroeconomics: Accommodating Frictions in Coordination", Handbook of Macroeconomics vol. 2, 1065-1240, 2016, DOI 10.1016/bs.hesmac.2016.04.011; also NBER WP 22297. A search-engine summary of the chapter reports that under incomplete information, higher-order beliefs react less to innovations than first-order beliefs; I did not open the chapter text to quote this. Chahrour and Jurado cite it as "a detailed review of the literature on dispersed information" and note (their footnote 13) that Angeletos and Lian also point out that Townsend's Sec. 8 economy observes prices with error. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/h/eee/macchp/v2-1065.html); [NBER WP 22297](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22297/w22297.pdf)
- Sargent (1991): "Equilibrium with signal extraction from endogenous variables", Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control 15(2), 245-273 (bibliographic details as given in both Chahrour-Jurado and Babichenko reference lists; not opened). — [Chahrour and Jurado 2025 references](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33794/w33794.pdf)

### Inferences
- The dividing line in the macro literature is exogenous versus endogenous signals, not the presence of higher-order beliefs. With exogenous signals the hierarchy is a linear projection problem and a finite state exists (Huo-Takayama). With endogenous signals (the signal process depends on the strategy profile), the equilibrium is a fixed point in the space of signal processes, and generically no finite ARMA representation exists (Huo-Takayama Section 5, Chahrour-Jurado Proposition 10).
- A continuous-time trading game in which the price carries the other trader's order flow is an endogenous-signal problem in exactly this sense. The macro literature offers no exact finite-dimensional solution for that class and treats it numerically by approximation.
- The macro methods (frequency domain, ARMA approximation) all presume stationarity and an infinite past, so they do not transfer directly to a finite-horizon Almgren-Chriss game.

### Gaps
- Townsend's own text could not be opened; the description of his assumptions and his Sec. 8 model comes from Chahrour and Jurado (2025), a secondary but detailed source.
- The Angeletos-Lian chapter text was not opened; the "higher-order beliefs react less" claim is from a search summary and should be quoted from the chapter before use.
- Whether Nimark (2017) was eventually published in a journal was not determined.

## Q2. Kyle-type models with several informed traders: how the hierarchy is closed, and what remains open

### Takeaway
Dynamic Kyle models with several informed traders are solved only under a symmetric static information structure (signals drawn once at time zero, identical variances and pairwise covariances): Foster-Viswanathan (1996, discrete time) and Back-Cao-Willard (2000, continuous time). The devices are the competitive market-maker pricing rule (price equals the conditional expectation of the value given order flow, which pins down the public belief), the symmetry assumption (which reduces the filter to one dimension), and a linear-strategy conjecture verified by a quadratic value function. Existence for Foster-Viswanathan was proved only in July 2026 (Choi and Larsen). Lambert-Ostrovsky-Panov (2018) allow arbitrary correlations but only in a single period. Asymmetric or continuously arriving private signals for several insiders are stated as not covered.

### Cited Findings
- Foster and Viswanathan (1996): F. Douglas Foster and S. Viswanathan, "Strategic Trading When Agents Forecast the Forecasts of Others", Journal of Finance 51(4), 1437-1478, September 1996, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb04075.x. Abstract: "a multiperiod model of trading with differentially informed traders, liquidity traders, and a marketmaker"; informed traders "not only compete with each other for trading profits, they also learn about other traders' signals from the observed order flow"; "the initial correlation among the informed traders' signals has a significant effect on the informed traders' profits and the informativeness of prices." — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:51:y:1996:i:4:p:1437-78); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb04075.x)
- The symmetry device, stated by Choi and Larsen (2026): "To reduce the model's complexity, Foster and Viswanathan (1996) place a symmetry assumption on the traders' information structure. The symmetry assumption produces a one dimensional second-order recursion." Footnote 1: "Foster and Viswanathan (1994) give an example of a Kyle model with an asymmetric information structure which leads to a two dimensional Kalman filter. Such extensions are not covered by our analysis." The information structure is: signals a_1..a_M jointly normal with identical variances and identical pairwise covariances, observed once at time zero; informed trader i observes a_i and past prices. — [Choi and Larsen, arXiv 2607.15057, 16 Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15057)
- Existence was open until 2026: "While both Kyle (1985) and Holden and Subrahmanyam (1992) give proofs ensuring that a Nash equilibrium exists, no existence proof is available to guarantee that the model in Foster and Viswanathan (1996) is supported by a Nash equilibrium." Choi and Larsen (Jin Hyuk Choi, UNIST; Kasper Larsen, Rutgers), "Existence and convergence of discrete-time Kyle models with multiple insiders", arXiv 2607.15057 [q-fin.TR], July 2026: "We resolve two long-standing open problems ... First, we prove that an equilibrium exists in the setting of Foster and Viswanathan (1996). Second, as the number of trading times goes to infinity, we prove that the discrete-time equilibrium converges to the continuous-time equilibrium already proven to exist in Back, Cao, and Willard (2000)." — [arXiv 2607.15057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15057)
- Back, Cao and Willard (2000): Kerry Back, C. Henry Cao, Gregory A. Willard, "Imperfect Competition among Informed Traders", Journal of Finance 55(5), 2117-2155, October 2000. Abstract: "We analyze competition among informed traders in the continuous-time Kyle (1985) model, as Foster and Viswanathan (1996) do in discrete time." When signals are imperfectly correlated they "explicitly describe the unique linear equilibrium"; they "confirm the conjecture of Holden and Subrahmanyam (1992) that there is no linear equilibrium when signals are perfectly correlated"; "the relatively large amount of private information remaining near the end of trading causes the market to approach complete illiquidity." — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v55y2000i5p2117-2155.html)
- Structure of the BCW equilibrium (as restated by Choi and Larsen, Theorem 3.1): the equilibrium is given by a scalar second-order ODE S''(t) = (4(M-1)S - 2)/(M S^2) (S')^2 with S(0) fixed and S(1) = 0; strategies are theta'_{j,s} = alpha(s) p_s + beta(s) a_j with deterministic alpha, beta, lambda. The market maker's zero-profit condition p_s = E[v | order flow] is imposed. — [arXiv 2607.15057, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15057)
- Lambert, Ostrovsky and Panov (2018): Nicolas S. Lambert, Michael Ostrovsky, Mikhail Panov, "Strategic Trading in Informationally Complex Environments", Econometrica 86(4), 1119-1157, July 2018, DOI 10.3982/ECTA12635; NBER WP 20516. The model is a single-period Kyle linear-normal model extended to "essentially arbitrary correlations among the random variables involved: the value of the traded asset, signals of strategic traders and market makers, and liquidity trader demand." Main result: "there always exists a unique linear equilibrium" and it is characterised analytically. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20516); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA12635)
- Bernhardt and Taub (2008): Dan Bernhardt and Bart Taub, "Cross-Asset Speculation in Stock Markets", Journal of Finance 63(5), 2385-2427, October 2008, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01400.x. Abstract: heterogeneously informed speculators trade several stocks, combining private information with information in prices, "taking into account how their trades influence the inferences of other speculators via prices"; "the covariance structure of asset fundamentals drives that of prices, while the covariance structure of liquidity trade drives that of order flows." The abstract does not describe the solution method; the frequency-domain (Wiener-Hopf) characterisation attributed to this paper in search summaries was not verified from the text. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v63y2008i5p2385-2427.html)
- Choi, Larsen and Seppi (2019): "Information and Trading Targets in a Dynamic Market Equilibrium", Journal of Financial Economics 132(3), 22-49; arXiv 1502.02083. Multi-period discrete-time Kyle market with an insider (knows v) and a rebalancer (private trading target a, correlated with v), noise traders and competitive market makers with p_n = E[v | y_1..y_n]. The paper states: "We also note that the insider's, market makers', and rebalancer's information sets are not nested." Listed extensions: "model trading in continuous-time", "multiple insiders and rebalancers". — [arXiv 1502.02083](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02083); [IDEAS/RePEc search listing](https://ideas.repec.org/)
- Dynamic private information for a single insider: Back and Pedersen (1998, Journal of Financial Markets 1(3-4), 385-402) and Danilova (2010, Stochastics 82(1), 111-131) treat "the case of dynamic private information where the insider's information evolves over time" (as cited by Kühn and Lorenz). I found no verified paper treating several insiders each with a continuously evolving private signal in a Kyle market. — [Kühn and Lorenz, arXiv 2312.00904, Introduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00904)
- Kühn and Lorenz (arXiv 2312.00904v2, July 2024), "Insider trading in discrete time Kyle games": existence of sequential equilibria for a single insider with a general finite information flow; it does not address multiple insiders. (Included because a search snippet attributed the "two open problems" result to the wrong paper; the correct source is Choi and Larsen 2026.) — [arXiv 2312.00904](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00904)
- Babichenko's own reading of this literature: "Kyle [18], Back [3], and Foster and Viswanathan [9] solve strategic trading problems with private information, each exploiting special structure (a single informed trader, correlated-signal symmetry) to close the belief hierarchy through model-specific conjectures." And: Section 5 of his paper targets "strategic trading with asymmetric private learning, a setting where guess-and-verify breaks down once traders differ in precision or learn continuously from private sources." — [Babichenko 2026, pp. 5, 25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)

### Inferences
- In all solved dynamic Kyle models with several insiders, three devices act together: (i) the market-maker pricing rule fixes the public belief as a function of order flow, so no agent needs beliefs about the market maker's beliefs; (ii) symmetry of the private signals makes each insider's belief about the others' signals a scalar function of one sufficient statistic; (iii) a linear-strategy ansatz with a quadratic value function lets the "hat" (equilibrium-path) processes stand in for the hierarchy.
- The open or unsolved cases, by the sources' own statements, are: asymmetric signal structures with several insiders (two-dimensional filters, "not covered"), several insiders with continuously arriving private signals, and continuous-time versions of the insider-plus-rebalancer model.
- None of these models has Almgren-Chriss-type price impact; the price is set by a market maker. The permanent-impact Almgren-Chriss price is a reduced-form substitute for the pricing rule and removes the market maker's belief, which is one reason the hierarchy does not close by the same route.

### Gaps
- The Bernhardt-Taub solution method (frequency domain, stationary, continuum of speculators) was not verified from the paper; only the abstract was accessible.
- No paper was found that treats several Kyle insiders with heterogeneous dynamic (OU) private signals; absence of evidence, not a proof of nonexistence.

## Q3. Continuous-time trading games with private signals: which device closes the hierarchy in each

### Takeaway
Every solved continuous-time trading game with differing filtrations closes the hierarchy by one of four devices: (a) a mean-field limit, in which no individual's information affects anyone else's signal (Casgrain-Jaimungal 2018, 2020; Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt 2024); (b) one-sided nested information, in which the less informed player's filtration is contained in the better informed player's (Wu-Jaimungal 2024); (c) observed or invertible actions, in which an agent can read the other's signal off the order flow it receives (Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt's traders observe the broker's speed; Donnelly-Li's brokers invert the informed flow); (d) zero-sum structure with a type drawn once (Cardaliaguet-Rainer 2009). Finite-player games with genuinely non-nested filtrations and impact from all players are characterised only abstractly by FBSDEs with projection operators (Evangelista-Thamsten), with existence under weak interaction and no computation of the filters.

### Cited Findings
- Casgrain and Jaimungal (2018), "Mean Field Games with Partial Information for Algorithmic Trading", arXiv 1803.04094v2 (March 2019). Information: "agent j [has] access to information about the paths of the price process S and their own inventory, but not others"; latent drift A and martingale M are unobserved. Device: "Since A and M are independent of the agent's initial inventories ... the projection of the dynamics of S onto the filtration F will be identical to the projection onto F^j" (Lemma 3.2 introduces A-hat_t = E[A_t | F_t], the same filter for every agent), and in the limit "each agent's individual contribution to nu^(N) vanishes ... each agent's objective functional no longer depends directly on nu^{-j}, but rather it depends on the population statistic". Closed-form MFG solution; epsilon-Nash for the finite game. — [arXiv 1803.04094](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04094)
- Casgrain and Jaimungal (2020), "Mean-Field Games with Differing Beliefs for Algorithmic Trading", Mathematical Finance 30(3), 995-1034; arXiv 1810.06101. Information: F^j is generated by the price, the average order flow of each sub-population, and own initial inventory (Section 2.3). Agents differ by belief measure P^k, not by private signal. On the finite game: "Obtaining this collection of strategies for the stochastic game with a finite number of players proves to be a difficult task ... One of the main obstacles in finding a solution to this problem is that each agent's strategy is adapted to different filtration F^j ... It is, however, possible to solve the stochastic game in the infinite population limit, and use the result as an approximation for the finite population game." The MFG FBSDE driver contains "the conditional expected value E^{P_k}[A^k_t + lambda nu-bar_t | F^j_t] ... This is a projection of the mean-fields onto the agent's filtration, and appears because the agent cannot directly observe the strategies of other individuals." Solved by LSMC. — [arXiv 1810.06101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06101)
- Bergault and Sanchez-Betancourt (2024), "A Mean Field Game between Informed Traders and a Broker", arXiv 2401.05257 (Jan 2024); published SIAM Journal on Financial Mathematics 16(2), 358-388, 2025 (publication record as cited by Donnelly and Li). Model: N informed traders, each observing a common OU signal alpha and a private OU signal alpha^n ("the private signal of one trader is hidden to every other trader"); one broker whose lit-market trading has linear permanent impact b on the price; traders' own trades have no price impact. Trader n's filtration is generated by S, alpha, alpha^n, Q^n, nu^B, k_n, sigma_n; the broker's by S, alpha, Q^B, nu^1..nu^N. On the finite game: "Solving this multi-agent (N + 1 players) problem boils down to the resolution of a system of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations, where the state variables are the inventory processes of the broker and the N informed traders, as well as their idiosyncratic and common signals. This system of N + 1 HJB equations is intractable in practice for a large number of informed traders." Device: mean-field limit with consistency nu-bar_t = E[nu^{I,*}_t | F^alpha_t]; closed form via Riccati ODEs. — [arXiv 2401.05257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05257)
- Wu and Jaimungal (2024), "Broker-Trader Partial Information Nash-Equilibria", arXiv 2412.17712v2 (April 2025). Information: the informed trader uses the full filtration F (including the signal alpha); the broker uses G, the filtration generated by prices only; G is "a closed subspace" situation, i.e. G is contained in F. Result: Nash equilibrium "corresponds to the solution of a nonstandard system of forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) that involves the two differing filtrations. For short enough time horizons, we prove that a unique solution of this system exists." Conclusion: "while we cannot solve the FBSDE system in closed-form, we are able to approximate the solution to arbitrary order in the impact of trading". The FBSDE (Theorem 4.2) contains the broker's projections alpha-hat = E[alpha | G], eta-hat, Q-hat^B, Q-hat^I. — [arXiv 2412.17712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17712)
- Cartea, Jaimungal and Sanchez-Betancourt, "Nash Equilibrium between Brokers and Traders", arXiv 2407.10561v3 (July 2025), forthcoming Finance and Stochastics (Springer DOI 10.1007/s00780-026-00595-7 appears in search results). This is the "perfect information Nash equilibrium": all players share one filtration; closed form via a non-Hermitian matrix Riccati equation. Included as the full-information benchmark for Wu-Jaimungal. — [arXiv 2407.10561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10561); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00780-026-00595-7)
- Evangelista and Thamsten, "On finite population games of optimal trading", arXiv 2004.00790v3 (Feb 2021). Model: N traders, each with own filtration F^i contained in F (no nesting assumed), permanent and temporary impact, running and terminal inventory penalties. The price "from the perspective of player i" is defined as ds^i_t = mu^i_t dt + alpha^i_t E[(1/N) sum_j nu^j_t | F^i_t] dt + dP^i_t, with the comment "the strategy nu^j of trader j need not be F^i-adapted; hence, the average ... is not necessarily F^i-adapted, whence we assume that player i projects it in the way we describe in (2.2)." Nash equilibrium characterised by an FBSDE containing the projection operator P_t(C_t x*_t) (Corollary 3.5); unique solution under the weak-interaction condition alpha-bar^2 < 16 kappa (lambda - beta-bar/(2N)) (Theorem 3.6, continuation method). With constant parameters and mu = 0 the average rate and inventory are deterministic and solve an ODE (Theorem 3.11). The paper does not compute the conditional expectations E[nu^j | F^i] for any stochastic private-signal example. — [arXiv 2004.00790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00790)
- Donnelly and Li, "Liquidity Competition Between Brokers and an Informed Trader", arXiv 2503.08287v2 (Nov 2025). N brokers, one informed trader with OU alpha. Device: the informed trader's optimal split across brokers satisfies kappa_i omega^i = kappa_j omega^j, so "when broker j supplies liquidity to the informed trader, she is able to deduce the total trading speed of the informed trader", and then alpha_t is recovered exactly by inverting the trader's linear strategy (eq. 15). Brokers also "observe all uninformed trading" and so deduce the other brokers' lit-market trades. Stackelberg structure; broker ODE system solved numerically. No filtering remains. — [arXiv 2503.08287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08287)
- Cardaliaguet and Rainer (2009), "Stochastic Differential Games with Asymmetric Information", Applied Mathematics and Optimization 59(1), 1-36, DOI 10.1007/s00245-008-9042-0. Abstract: "a two-player zero-sum stochastic differential game in which the players have an asymmetric information on the random payoff"; "the game has a value and characterize this value in terms of dual viscosity solutions of some second order Hamilton-Jacobi equation." A later paper (Cardaliaguet and Rainer, "Pathwise strategies for stochastic differential games with an erratum to 'Stochastic differential games with asymmetric information'", AMO 2013) corrects some statements. — [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/21241904); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00245-008-9042-0); [Erratum, arXiv 1212.4634](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.4634)
- Mean-field games as a device, in Babichenko's words: "Mean-field games take large-population limits in which no single agent's action measurably affects any other agent's signal ... The bilateral informational externality is absent by construction in the mean-field limit". And on the common-information approach: it "resolves [asymmetric information] through a coordinator's beliefs; the channel by which player i's action shifts player k's posterior, which shifts k's action, which shifts i's signal, is not captured." — [Babichenko 2026, pp. 4-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)

### Inferences
- The two Toronto MFG papers and Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt all state explicitly that the finite-player game with differing filtrations is the hard case and that the mean-field limit is adopted to avoid it.
- Wu-Jaimungal is the only verified finite-player continuous-time trading game with genuinely different filtrations that is solved beyond an abstract characterisation, and it relies on nesting (the broker's information is a sub-filtration of the trader's), small horizon, and a series expansion rather than a closed form.
- Evangelista-Thamsten's FBSDE is the abstract form of the target problem. Its projection operator is exactly the object a learned-strategy method would avoid computing. Their existence result shows the equilibrium is well posed under weak interaction, but offers no algorithm for the projections when signals are private and stochastic.
- Cardaliaguet-Rainer's belief-as-state method works because the game is zero-sum and the private type is drawn once; it does not extend in that form to non-zero-sum games with continuously arriving signals (no source claims such an extension).

### Gaps
- Nadtochiy and Shkolnikov: no paper on partial-information or asymmetric-information games by these two authors was found. Their joint work located is "Mean field systems on networks, with singular interaction through hitting times" (Annals of Probability), which is unrelated. The reference in the task may be misattributed; treat it as unverified.
- Carmona and Delarue: their two-volume book (Probabilistic Theory of Mean Field Games with Applications, Springer 2018) is cited by Babichenko for LQG MFGs with common noise; I did not find or open a Carmona-Delarue treatment of MFGs with private signals. Firoozi and Caines (IEEE CDC 2015, 2016) on partially observed MFGs are cited by both Casgrain-Jaimungal and Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt but were not opened.
- Cardaliaguet and Rainer's companion paper "On a continuous-time game with incomplete information" (Mathematics of Operations Research 2009) was not opened.
- Cont, Micheli and Neuman, "Fast and slow optimal trading with exogenous information" (Finance and Stochastics 2025) appeared in search results and may be relevant to asymmetric information between two traders; not opened.

## Q4. Babichenko, arXiv 2603.12140: existence, content, computability

### Takeaway
The paper exists (Sam Babichenko, UC Santa Barbara, "Forecasting and Manipulating the Forecasts of Others", arXiv 2603.12140v3 [math.OC], 23 March 2026; arXiv only, no journal found). It treats finite-player continuous-time LQG games in which each player's control enters the state and each player observes the state through a private noisy channel. The device is to condition on the primitive Brownian paths ("noise-state") rather than on the state: within the class of "noise-state linear" strategies with deterministic kernels, filtering and best responses stay in the class, and the equilibrium is a deterministic fixed point in impulse-response kernels on the triangle {0 <= u <= t <= T}, which is computed by Picard iteration on a grid. Uniqueness is proved only for short horizons; existence for all horizons; whether the kernel class is without loss of generality is stated as the main open question. The Kyle-type trading embedding (Section 5) gives only a first-order condition; I found no numerical results for it.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic record and claim: "We provide the first exact equilibrium characterization of finite-player continuous-time linear-quadratic games with endogenous signals. Estimating primitive shocks rather than the state collapses the belief hierarchy onto deterministic impulse-response maps, reducing Nash equilibrium to a deterministic fixed point with no truncation and no large-population limit." Author affiliation: Department of Statistics and Applied Probability, UCSB; advisor Tomoyuki Ichiba; replication code at github.com/sbabichenko/Noise-State-Games (not opened). — [arXiv 2603.12140v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Model (Section 3): n players, state dX_t = (A(t) X_t + sum_i D^i_t) dt + Sigma(t) dW^0_t; player i observes dY^i_t = Gamma^i(t) X_t dt + E^i dW_t, where E^i selects the i-th block of the stacked primitive noise W = (W^0, ..., W^n); quadratic costs with possibly stochastic linear coefficients; admissible controls are F^i-adapted with F^i_t = sigma(Y^i_s: s <= t). Nash equilibrium is defined by holding opponents' strategy maps (not realised actions) fixed under deviations: "changing one player's control law changes the state dynamics, which changes every opponent's signal, filtering problem, and realized action, even though their decision rules have not changed." — [arXiv 2603.12140v3, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- The noise-state: W-hat^i_t(u) := E[W_u | F^i_t], 0 <= u <= t, "the running conditional mean of the entire primitive noise path". Strategies: D^i_t = D-bar^i_t + integral_0^t D^i_t(u) d_u W-hat^i_t(u) with deterministic mean D-bar and deterministic kernel D^i_t(u). "The key structural result (Corollary 4.5) is that the best response to any such strategy profile, over all admissible controls, is itself of this form." — [arXiv 2603.12140v3, Section 2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Why the regress closes (Remark 4.1): "Discretizing time, each observation increment is affine in the noise increments, so by joint Gaussianity the conditional mean E[Delta W | Delta Y^i] is a deterministic linear projection; each layer of 'beliefs about beliefs' is a further deterministic composition of projections, which is why the regress closes." — [arXiv 2603.12140v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Main theorems: Theorem 4.1 (Linear Filtering Closure) gives the deterministic filtering kernel F^i_t(u, s) explicitly and the unresolved kernel X-tilde^i; Theorem 4.2 derives a backward system for the physical costate H^{X,i} and belief adjoints H^{k,i}_t(u) with the "information wedge" V^i_t = sum_{k != i} P^k(t) integral_0^t X-tilde^k_t(z) H^{k,i}_t(z) dz; Corollary 4.3: V^i vanishes if controls do not enter the state; Proposition 4.6: with n >= 2 and endogenous signals, the policy kernel does not factor as S(t) X_t(u) (certainty equivalence fails). — [arXiv 2603.12140v3, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Well-posedness: Theorem D.5 (Short-horizon equilibrium): "there exists T* > 0 ... such that for T in (0, T*] [the best-response map] is a contraction ... there exists a unique Nash equilibrium in noise-state linear strategies with bounded kernels, Picard iterates converge geometrically, and the equilibrium is Nash over the full admissible L2 class." Theorem D.6 (Global existence): for every T > 0 a Nash equilibrium exists (Schauder). Remark D.1: "Theorem D.6 gives existence but not uniqueness for T > T*." — [arXiv 2603.12140v3, Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Main open question (Section 6): "The main open question is whether the deterministic impulse-response class is without loss of generality." Also listed: "Stochastic coefficients (Remark D.2) and global uniqueness are natural next steps". — [arXiv 2603.12140v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Computation (Section 2.3, two-player example): "Equilibrium is computed by iterating the best-response operator on the deterministic impulse-response system (Section 4), discretized on a uniform grid of N = 40 points; no Monte Carlo simulation is needed. Parameters: T = 1, x_0 = 0, r = 0.1, no terminal cost. The Picard residual reaches 10^-5 in 19 iterations (about 10 ms in C++), decaying geometrically. At longer horizons the binding constraint is numerical resolution (the filtering kernel develops near-singularities) rather than economic." — [arXiv 2603.12140v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Trading embedding (Section 5): n informed traders, a competitive market maker, fundamental V_t = sigma_V W^V_t; "All players observe aggregate order flow dZ_t = sum_j D^j_t dt + sigma_0 dW^0_t; trader i additionally observes a private signal dY^i_t = Gamma^i(t) V_t dt + dW^i_t. The market maker observes only Z and posts the competitive price P_t := E[V_T | F^0_t]." The profit is linear in D^i plus a quadratic form through a deterministic price-impact kernel l(t, s); the first-order condition is an integral equation (5.3) whose time derivative gives lambda(t) D^i_t = -(d/dt) H^{i,val}_t + E[integral_t^T (partial l / partial t)(tau, t) D^i_tau d tau | F^i_t] (5.4). The retrieved pages contain no numerical results for n >= 2 traders. — [arXiv 2603.12140v3, Section 5 and Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Citation errors in the paper's reference list: Kasa (2000) is cited with the wrong journal and DOI (see Q1). Huo and Takayama is cited as "Working paper, 2023" although it appeared in RESTUD 92(5), 2025. — [arXiv 2603.12140v3 references](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140); [RESTUD record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/restud/v92y2025i5p3138-3173..html)

### Inferences
- The method is computable for LQG games: the unknowns are deterministic kernels on a time triangle, so the cost scales with the grid (N^2 per kernel per player, with nested integrals), not with Monte Carlo. The reported example is two scalar players with a 40-point grid.
- The result is a linear-Gaussian result: it needs linear dynamics, Gaussian primitive noise, quadratic objectives, and (for the trading embedding) a market maker whose pricing is a deterministic linear functional of order flow. It does not cover nonlinear filters (e.g. Markov-chain latent factors) or non-quadratic objectives.
- Two limitations matter for the comparison with a learned-strategy method: equilibrium is proved unique only for short horizons, and optimality is proved only against the noise-state linear class with a separate argument that best responses stay in the class; whether non-linear strategies could do better is unresolved by the author's own statement.
- An Almgren-Chriss game with private OU alphas and a public price with permanent impact is an LQG problem of the form in Section 3 with one public and one private channel per player; the paper's Appendix E stacking argument suggests it could be embedded, but the paper does not do so.

### Gaps
- Whether the paper has been submitted or accepted anywhere was not determined; only the arXiv record exists in the sources searched.
- The GitHub replication repository was not opened; whether it contains code for the n-trader Kyle embedding is unknown.
- Version history (v1, v2 dates) was not retrieved; only v3 (23 March 2026) was read.

## Q5. Decentralised LQG and team theory with non-classical information (Witsenhausen 1968, Ho-Chu 1972, Nayyar-Mahajan-Teneketzis 2013): what is solvable and what "non-nested" implies

### Takeaway
For LQG teams, linear strategies are optimal if and only if the information structure is partially nested (whoever's action affects your observation has information contained in yours); Witsenhausen's 1968 counterexample shows that a two-stage LQG team with a non-nested structure has a nonlinear optimum and that the problem is not solvable in closed form. The common-information approach gives a dynamic program for decentralised control when there is shared information, but it resolves beliefs through a fictitious coordinator and does not capture the strategic belief-manipulation channel of a game. A two-trader game in which each trader's orders move the price the other observes, without sharing the signal that generated them, is non-nested.

### Cited Findings
- Witsenhausen (1968): H. S. Witsenhausen, "A Counterexample in Stochastic Optimum Control", SIAM Journal on Control 6(1), 131-147, 1968. The counterexample is an LQG two-stage team in which "the first action affects the second observation without making the first controller's information available to the second", and nonlinear strategies strictly outperform the best linear one. — [PDF of the original article](http://www.control.ece.ntua.gr/UndergraduateCourses/StochElegx/H.%20S.%20WITSENHAUSEN.pdf); [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Counterexample-in-Stochastic-Optimum-Control-Witsenhausen/4dc68d850735017654727914103cd38387eadc5b)
- Ho and Chu (1972): Y.-C. Ho and K.-C. Chu, "Team Decision Theory and Information Structures in Optimal Control Problems, Part I", IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control AC-17(1), 15-22, February 1972. Definition 3: "An information structure (3) is called partially nested if j precedent of i implies Z_j subset of Z_i for all i, j, and gamma in Gamma." Theorem 2: "In a dynamic team with partially nested information structure, the optimal control for each member exists, is unique and linear in z_i." Informal statement: "if a DM's action affects our information, then knowing what he knows will yield linear optimal solutions." — [Ho and Chu 1972, PDF](https://mcrotk.github.io/courses/references/ho_chu.pdf)
- Ho and Chu on games (Remark 1 after Theorem 1): "The reduction of (26) to (27) is possible only when we are considering pure strategy solution exclusively. In more general game-theoretic optimization problems with different information for each member in a team and different payoff functions for each of the teams, partially nested information structure alone may not be sufficient to effect the reduction." Application 4 shows that LQG zero-sum multistage games with each player having only its own observations (not partially nested) still have linear saddle points, "primarily due to the absence of cooperation". — [Ho and Chu 1972, PDF](https://mcrotk.github.io/courses/references/ho_chu.pdf)
- Ho and Chu on why dynamic teams are hard (Section I): "in a dynamic team, present information is affected by what has been done in the past. Therefore, the present estimation and decision are dependent on the actions of the other members in the past; this very dependence is itself affected by the past actions which are part of the solution to be obtained." — [Ho and Chu 1972, PDF](https://mcrotk.github.io/courses/references/ho_chu.pdf)
- Nayyar, Mahajan and Teneketzis (2013): "Decentralized Stochastic Control with Partial History Sharing: A Common Information Approach", IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control 58(7), 1644-1658. The idea is "to formulate an equivalent centralized stochastic control problem from the point of view of a fictitious coordinator that observes only the information that is commonly available to all decision-makers", yielding a dynamic programming decomposition for the partial-history-sharing class. — [arXiv 1209.1695](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1695); [Springer book chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-02150-8_4)
- Babichenko's assessment of the common-information approach for games: "The approach handles asymmetric information but resolves it through a coordinator's beliefs; the channel by which player i's action shifts player k's posterior, which shifts k's action, which shifts i's signal, is not captured." And of team theory: "Team-theoretic solutions bypass the belief hierarchy because a social planner with aligned objectives solves a single-agent control problem—the fixed-point structure never arises." — [Babichenko 2026, pp. 4-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Characterisation of non-classical structures (from a 2026 survey of Witsenhausen's counterexample): "For a fixed (nonclassical) information structure, there are instances (depending on the structure of the objective function) when linear policies are optimal and other instances (including Witsenhausen's counterexample) when the optimal policies are nonlinear." — [arXiv 2610.25853](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25853)

### Inferences
- In the target game, trader 1's orders enter the price that trader 2 observes, and trader 2 does not observe trader 1's alpha. By Ho-Chu's Definition 3 the structure is not partially nested. Linear optimality is therefore not guaranteed by team theory, and even in the team (cooperative) version Witsenhausen's example shows nonlinear strategies may be strictly better.
- For games, Ho-Chu's Remark 1 says partial nesting is not even sufficient; the zero-sum case is the exception. Babichenko's result is consistent with this: linear (noise-state linear) equilibria are shown to exist, but optimality against nonlinear deviations is proved only as "best response stays in the class", with the class itself not shown to be exhaustive.
- The common-information approach would apply only if the traders shared some history (e.g. the price path), and then it reduces the problem to a coordinator choosing partial strategies as functions of private information; that is a dynamic program over function-valued actions, which is computationally the same obstacle a learned-strategy method is meant to handle directly.

### Gaps
- The Nayyar-Mahajan-Teneketzis text was not opened beyond the abstract; whether it discusses games (as opposed to teams) was not checked.
- No source was found that states a clean "non-nested implies no finite-dimensional sufficient statistic" theorem for non-zero-sum LQG games; the implication is assembled from Ho-Chu, Witsenhausen, and the Huo-Takayama and Chahrour-Jurado infinite-state results.

## Q6. Is a finite-player game with two traders, each observing a private OU signal and a common price with permanent impact from both, solved anywhere?

### Takeaway
No. No verified source solves, in closed form or numerically, a finite-player Almgren-Chriss-type game in which each of two traders observes a private OU alpha and a common price carrying permanent impact from both. The closest solved models each remove one ingredient: Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt (private OU alphas and a common impacted price, but impact only from an observed broker and solved only in the mean-field limit); Wu-Jaimungal (two players, one price, but one-sided nested information and no closed form); Evangelista-Thamsten (finite N, arbitrary filtrations, impact from all, but only an abstract FBSDE with projection operators); Babichenko (finite n, private continuous signals, endogenous signals, computable kernel fixed point, but LQG with a market-maker price and no Almgren-Chriss example); Back-Cao-Willard and Choi-Larsen (several insiders in continuous time, but static symmetric signals and market-maker pricing).

### Cited Findings
- Bergault and Sanchez-Betancourt (2024): the N-player model has exactly the private OU signals (dalpha^n = -k_n alpha^n dt + sigma_n dW^n, hidden from others) and a common price with permanent impact, but the impact is the broker's (dS = (b nu^B + alpha^n + alpha) dt + sigma_S dW under P^{n, nu^B}) and each trader observes nu^B. The finite game is declared "intractable in practice" and only the MFG is solved. — [arXiv 2401.05257, Sections 2.1-2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05257)
- Wu and Jaimungal (2024): two players, OU signal known to one and filtered by the other, impact from the broker only; existence and uniqueness for small T; series solution in the impact parameter. — [arXiv 2412.17712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17712)
- Evangelista and Thamsten (2021): N players with arbitrary filtrations F^i, permanent impact from all; the equilibrium FBSDE is written with E[sum_j nu^j | F^i]; existence and uniqueness under weak interaction; no example with stochastic private signals is computed. — [arXiv 2004.00790, Sections 2-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00790)
- Babichenko (2026): the general model covers n players with private noisy observations of a state driven by all controls; the two-player numerical example has symmetric players each observing a private diffusion signal of a common state X driven by both controls (dX = (D^1 + D^2) dt + dW^0, dY^i = sqrt(p) X dt + dW^i), with tracking costs, not trading costs. The trading embedding uses a Kyle market maker, not permanent Almgren-Chriss impact, and no numerical results for it were found. — [arXiv 2603.12140v3, Sections 2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Back, Cao and Willard (2000) and Choi and Larsen (2026): M >= 2 insiders in continuous time with market-maker pricing; signals drawn once at t = 0 with identical variances and covariances; closed-form via a scalar ODE. Asymmetric signal structures "are not covered". — [IDEAS/RePEc BCW](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v55y2000i5p2117-2155.html); [arXiv 2607.15057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15057)
- Choi, Larsen and Seppi (2019): two strategic traders with non-nested information in a discrete-time Kyle market, computed numerically by a per-period polynomial system; continuous time and multiple insiders left for future work. — [arXiv 1502.02083](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02083)
- Casgrain and Jaimungal (2020) on the finite-player case with differing filtrations: "Obtaining this collection of strategies for the stochastic game with a finite number of players proves to be a difficult task ... each agent's strategy is adapted to different filtration F^j". — [arXiv 1810.06101, p. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06101)
- Two-player optimal-execution games with private information in the existing finance literature enter private information only through trading targets drawn at time zero (Choi, Larsen and Seppi, "Smart TWAP trading in continuous-time equilibria", cited by Evangelista-Thamsten as reference [21]) or assume a common signal (Micheli, Muhle-Karbe and Neuman, "Closed-loop Nash competition for liquidity", arXiv 2112.02961, appeared in search results; not opened). — [arXiv 2004.00790 references](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00790)

### Inferences
- The target model sits at the intersection of two things that the solved literature keeps apart: finite N with impact from every player (Evangelista-Thamsten, closed-loop execution games) and private continuously arriving signals (Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt, Wu-Jaimungal). When both are present, the price is an endogenous signal in the Huo-Takayama and Chahrour-Jurado sense, and the macro results say to expect no finite-dimensional state for the filters.
- Within the LQG case (OU alphas, Brownian price noise, quadratic costs), Babichenko's kernel method is the only verified exact approach that applies in principle. It would deliver equilibrium kernels on a time grid, subject to its short-horizon uniqueness and class restriction. This is the natural benchmark against which a learned-strategy solution should be checked, and it is also the method's boundary: a learned strategy is not restricted to the noise-state linear class, which is relevant precisely because Ho-Chu and Witsenhausen show linear strategies need not be optimal with non-nested information.
- Outside the LQG case (Markov-chain latent alphas, nonlinear impact, non-quadratic penalties), no exact method was found; the mean-field limit is the only solved route, and it removes the bilateral belief channel by construction.

### Gaps
- Micheli-Muhle-Karbe-Neuman (arXiv 2112.02961) and Cont-Micheli-Neuman (Finance and Stochastics 2025) were not opened; both appear to assume a common signal, but this should be confirmed before they are cited as "common-information only".
- The Smart TWAP paper (Choi, Larsen, Seppi) was not opened; its information structure (private targets, Kyle pricing) is taken from Evangelista-Thamsten's description.
- A negative result ("no closed form exists") is not available anywhere; the claim that the two-trader private-OU-alpha permanent-impact game is unsolved rests on the absence of a solution in the sources above and on the explicit statements of intractability in Bergault-Sanchez-Betancourt and Casgrain-Jaimungal.
