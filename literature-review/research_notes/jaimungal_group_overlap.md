# Overlap between "deep Picard regression" and Jaimungal-group work on games with partial information

Research notes, compiled 18 November 2026. Every identifier below was opened and checked (arXiv abstract page or full PDF via alphaXiv). Quotes are verbatim from the papers; the "Inferences" subsections are my own reading and are marked as such.

Reference point for all comparisons (the proposed method, as described in the assignment): N-player LQ stochastic differential game; player i acts on its own observation filtration F^i, non-nested across players (e.g. two traders each with a private OU alpha and a common impacted price); first-order condition u^i_t = (1/2a_i) E[Lambda^i_t | F^i_t] with Lambda^i an explicit pathwise functional of the future; simulate paths under current controls, evaluate Lambda^i, fit a GRU per player on its own observation increments by least squares to a damped target, iterate (damped Picard), average iterates; no filter and no belief hierarchy is computed.

## Key question 1: Antunes, Saporito, Jaimungal, arXiv 2512.14967

### Takeaway
Verified. "Deep Learning and Elicitability for McKean-Vlasov FBSDEs With Common Noise" (v1 16 Dec 2025, v2 11 Jun 2026, v3 10 Oct 2026; cs.LG, q-fin.CP, q-fin.MF; 28 pages). It does do damped Picard iteration with a recurrent network regressed on a sub-filtration (the common-noise filtration F^0) inside a mean-field fixed-point loop, so the computational template overlaps substantially with the proposed method. It does not treat finite-player games, does not treat non-nested or player-specific information, and the sub-filtration it conditions on is the single common-noise filtration of a representative agent, not each player's own observation filtration. Its examples are the Carmona-Fouque-Sun systemic-risk model (with a quantile variant) and an Aiyagari-Bewley-Huggett growth model.

- Abstract (verbatim): "We present a novel numerical method for solving McKean-Vlasov forward-backward stochastic differential equations (MV--FBSDEs) with common noise, combining Picard iterations, elicitability and deep learning. ### Inferences
- The computational core is the same as the proposed method's: freeze the other unknowns, simulate paths, form a pathwise target, regress a recurrent network on a sub-filtration by least squares (quadratic score = L2 projection), soft-update with a damping weight, repeat. The paper even frames the L2 projection as "elicitability of the mean" and parameterizes the conditional expectation as an RNN on the path of the conditioning noise. A reviewer will see this as the nearest prior art.
- The differences are structural, not cosmetic: (i) one representative agent and one conditioning filtration F^0 (common noise) rather than N players each with its own F^i; (ii) the mean-field fixed point is over the law (through S_t), not over N best responses; (iii) they solve the BSDE for the adjoint (Y, Z, Z^0) with a decoupling-field network, whereas the proposed method regresses the control directly from an explicit first-order condition and never introduces Z; (iv) nothing in the paper is non-nested: every agent observes X_t and W^0, and F^0 ⊂ full filtration; (v) no claim or experiment on finite N.
- The error-bound section in v3 (added after v1) shows the group is now interested in convergence theory for damped Picard + regression; a finite-N, multi-filtration version would be a natural follow-up for them, which raises the priority of positioning the proposed method clearly against this paper.
- The "elicitability" framing is more general than L2 regression (quantiles, expected shortfall). The proposed method uses only the quadratic score, so it does not subsume theirs on that axis.

## Key question 2: Wu and Jaimungal, arXiv 2412.17712

### Takeaway
Verified. "Broker-Trader Partial Information Nash-Equilibria" (v1 23 Dec 2024, v2 1 Apr 2025; q-fin.MF, math.PR, q-fin.TR; no venue listed). Two-player nested-information game: the informed trader sees the full filtration F, the broker sees only the price filtration G ⊂ F. Nothing is solved numerically. Theorem 4.1 is a Banach fixed-point contraction for small T on the product of H^2_G × H^2_F × ...; Theorem 4.2 characterizes the equilibrium as a "filtered" FBSDE across two filtrations; Section 5 gives a power series in the transient-impact strength h with closed-form coefficients. The two-way private (non-nested) case and a deep learning solver are both named as future work.

### Inferences
- The fixed-point map Φ in Theorem 4.1 is precisely "each player's control = (1/2a) E[pathwise functional | own filtration]", i.e. the same Picard map the proposed method discretizes and regresses. The theorem is a two-player, nested-information, small-T contraction for that map. The proposed method's non-nested N-player setting is strictly outside the theorem's scope but uses the same operator.
- The paper's own future-work list names two of the proposed method's contributions as open: "multiple informed traders with differing information sets" and "a deep learning algorithm to approximate the FBSDE system". The 2025 GERAD abstract shows the authors intend to pursue the deep-learning direction; no preprint of it was found as of 18 Nov 2026 (see Gaps).
- The nesting G ⊂ F is used in the proof of Theorem 4.2 (e.g. eq. (4.8) E[η_t|G_t] = E[η̂_t|G_t] and the projection tricks). A non-nested two-trader case would require a genuinely different argument; the proposed method's numerical approach does not need it.

## Key question 3: Casgrain and Jaimungal, "Mean-field games with differing beliefs for algorithmic trading", Mathematical Finance 2020

### Takeaway
Verified: arXiv 1810.06101, Mathematical Finance 30(3):995-1034, 2020. Partial information is handled by a common price filtration F plus each agent's own inventory (F^j ⊃ F); the latent drift is filtered explicitly (E^{P_k}[A^k_t | F_t], Markov-chain example with Wonham-type posterior). Equilibrium is the MFG limit solved by ansatz + Riccati ODE + a non-standard linear BSDE; the finite-player game is not solved but an ε-Nash theorem is proved; a least-squares Monte Carlo method computes the conditional expectations. Information is symmetric across agents up to private initial inventory, so this is not a non-nested private-signal game.

## Key question 5: Bergault and Sánchez-Betancourt, arXiv 2401.05257

### Takeaway
Verified. "A Mean Field Game between Informed Traders and a Broker" (v1 10 Jan 2024; q-fin.TR, math.OC); published as SIAM J. Financial Mathematics 16(2):358-388, 2025 (per citations in Aqsha-Drissi-Sánchez-Betancourt). Finite-player model has N informed traders each with a common OU signal α and a private OU signal α^n, plus a broker; but the finite game is declared intractable and only the mean-field limit is solved, in closed form via Gâteaux derivatives, FBSDE, and a (nonsymmetric) matrix Riccati equation. In the MFG the admissible set is F-progressively measurable for the canonical filtration of the representative agent and the broker uses the common signal; no filtering is done. Numerical section plots coefficient functions and sample paths.

## Key question 6: Firoozi and Caines, arXiv 1810.04369

### Takeaway
Verified. "ε-Nash Equilibria for Major Minor LQG Mean Field Games with Partial Observations of All Agents" (v1 10 Oct 2018, v4 10 Sep 2020; math.OC, eess.SY; "To appear in the IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control"). Solves the infinite-population major-minor LQG MFG with noisy observations by Kalman filters and the Separation Principle; each minor agent recursively estimates the major agent's estimate of its own state (a second-order belief), and the asymmetry stops the regress. The finite-population partially observed game is stated to be an open problem; the simulation (100 minor agents) applies the MFG control laws, not a finite-N equilibrium.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: Dena Firoozi (HEC Montréal) and Peter E. Caines (McGill); v1 10 Oct 2018, v2 28 Mar 2019, v3 5 Feb 2020, v4 10 Sep 2020; math.OC, eess.SY; comments "To appear in the IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control". — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)
- Abstract (verbatim): "The partially observed major minor LQG and nonlinear mean field game (PO MM LQG MFG) systems where it is assumed the major agent's state is partially observed by each minor agent, and the major agent completely observes its own state have been analysed in the literature. In this paper, PO MM LQG MFG problems with general information patterns are studied where (i) the major agent has partial observations of its own state, and (ii) each minor agent has partial observations of its own state and the major agent's state. The assumption of partial observations by all agents leads to a new situation involving the recursive estimation by each minor agent of the major agent's estimate of its own state. For a general case of indefinite LQG MFG systems, the existence of ε-Nash equilibria together with the individual agents' control laws yielding the equilibria are established via the Separation Principle." — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)
- Belief hierarchy and why it stops (p. 3, p. 15): "We remark that an infinite regress does not happen here due to the asymmetric major minor (MM) feature of the MFG problem." and Observation 1: "By virtue of the asymmetric information available to the major agent and a generic minor agent, an infinite regress does not occur in the process of estimating other agents' states. In fact to calculate the best response action, the major agent only estimates its own state and hence does not estimate minor agents' states, while each minor agent estimates its own state and the major agent's state." Filter (45) produces "(x̂_{0|F^y_0})_{|F^y_i} := E{x̂_{0|F^y_0} | F^y_i}, denotes the minor agent A_i's estimate of the major agent's estimate of its own state." — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)

Theorem 3: "the KF-MFG state estimation scheme (27)-(31) and (48)-(51) together with the Consistency Condition equations ... generate an infinite family of stochastic control laws ... (iii) ... yields a unique ε-Nash equilibrium within the class of linear control laws U^{N,L}_y". — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)

- Conclusion (p. 27): "the dynamic game theoretic equilibrium which is established in this paper constitutes a rare case wherein agents explicitly generate estimates of another agent's beliefs. Moreover, this does not give rise to an infinite regress due to the information asymmetry of the major and minor agents." — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)
- Related Firoozi-Caines-Jaimungal paper, verified: "Mean Field Game Systems with Common Noise and Markovian Latent Processes", arXiv 1809.07865 (v3 23 Jul 2019). Information is common: Assumptions 3-4 "The major agent A_0 completely observes its own state x^0_t, and the common process y_t" and "Each minor agent A_i ... completely observes its own state x^i_t, the major agent's state x^0_t, and the common process y_t"; "the Wonham filtering method is used to estimate the distribution of Γ_t based on the observations of each agent on y_t". Solved in closed form (Riccati + offset BSDE) with ε-Nash (Theorem 3); no numerics in the served text. — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1809.07865)

### Inferences
- Firoozi-Caines is the control-theory formalization of the problem the proposed method sidesteps: with non-nested private observations the Kalman-filter route produces beliefs about beliefs; they escape only through the major-minor asymmetry and the infinite-population limit, and they state in print that the finite-population partially observed case is open. - The Macrì-Jaimungal-Lillo conclusion ("multi-agent settings ... heterogeneous information") and the Wu-Jaimungal conclusion ("multiple informed traders with differing information sets ... deep learning algorithm") together indicate the group views the proposed setting as the next step. Timeliness risk is real: a 2027 preprint from the group on this exact problem would not be surprising.

## Key question 8: Overall overlap assessment (synthesis)

### Takeaway
The proposed method's algorithmic template (damped Picard, RNN regressed on a sub-filtration by L2 to a pathwise target, iterate averaging) is already in print for the common-noise MFG case (Antunes-Saporito-Jaimungal 2512.14967, first posted Dec 2025). Its theoretical fixed-point map is already in print for the two-player nested case (Wu-Jaimungal 2412.17712, Theorem 4.1). What is not in print anywhere found: finite-N, non-nested, per-player observation filtrations, controls learned directly from the first-order condition without a filter, belief hierarchy, or adjoint BSDE.

### Cited Findings
- Antunes et al. regress "s ∈ L^2[F^0_t] as a recurrent neural network s(t, (W^0_s)_{s≤t})" and damp with "Ψ^{k+1} = δ Ψ^k + (1 − δ) Ψ̂^{k+1}". — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2512.14967)
- Wu-Jaimungal's contraction map is "Φ_{t,1}(Υ) = (1/2) a^{-1} E[... | G_t]", "Φ_{t,2}(Υ) = (1/2) b^{-1} E[... | F_t]" with "H^2_G is a closed subspace of H^2_F". — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.17712)
- Wu-Jaimungal name "multiple informed traders with differing information sets" and "a deep learning algorithm to approximate the FBSDE system" as future work. — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2412.17712)
- Bergault-Sánchez-Betancourt call the N-player private-signal game "intractable in practice for a large number of informed traders". — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2401.05257)
- Firoozi-Caines call the finite-population partially observed game "an open problem" because of "second-order and higher-order estimates". — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/1810.04369)
- Hambly-Xu-Yang: "stochastic games with private beliefs have been identified as an open problem in the past decade". — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2307.15842)
