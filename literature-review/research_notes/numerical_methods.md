Date of search: 2026-11-18. Every arXiv identifier below was opened (abstract page or full text). Items marked "unverified" were not opened and come from search snippets or memory.

Criteria used in the comparison: (a) finite players, (b) continuous time, (c) partial/private information, (d) non-nested information across players, (e) no explicit filter or belief hierarchy computed, and the validation examples.

The two closest in information structure are Babichenko (continuous time, finite n, non-nested, LQ, Picard/best-response iteration, but deterministic filter kernels and restricted to linear-Gaussian noise-state strategies) and Guan–Shames–Summers (non-nested, best-response iteration, but discrete time, zero-sum, two players, Kalman belief hierarchy). ## Deep fictitious play and variants: do any treat partial or asymmetric information?

### Takeaway
No. All deep fictitious play papers (Hu 2019/2021; Han–Hu 2020; Han–Hu–Long 2021) assume full information: either open-loop controls adapted to the joint Brownian filtration or Markovian feedback on the common state. Ruimeng Hu's recent partial-information games (2025–2026) are two-player with hidden cost parameters and do not compute a partial-information Nash equilibrium; they average the full-information equilibrium over a prior.

### Cited Findings
- Hu, "Deep fictitious play for stochastic differential games", arXiv 1903.09376 (v3, 3 Sep 2020), published Commun. Math. Sci. 19(2):325–353, 2021. Abstract: "we apply the idea of fictitious play to design deep neural networks (DNNs), and develop deep learning theory and algorithms for computing the Nash equilibrium of asymmetric N-player non-zero-sum stochastic differential games ... we prove the convergence of fictitious play under appropriate assumptions, and verify that the convergent limit forms an open-loop Nash equilibrium." — [arXiv 1903.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09376); journal reference from the citation list of [arXiv 2605.12950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12950) and the NSF PAR copy ([par.nsf.gov](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10253641)).
- Information structure in Hu (2021): "depending on the space where one searches for actions (the information structure available to the players), the types of equilibria include open-loop (W[0,t]), closed-loop (X[0,t]), and closed-loop in feedback form (Xt). We start with the setup (1.3) which corresponds to the open-loop case." The networks are fed "the initial states X0 and noises W[0,t] for each player's network, seeking for open-loop Nash equilibrium." Validation: "Numerical results are shown in three examples of N = 5, 10, 24 players" on the Carmona–Fouque–Sun LQ interbank game; "Max Relative Err" 1.15%, 2.45%, 2.95%. — [arXiv 1903.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09376)
- Hu (2021) convergence condition for LQ fictitious play: "the family {α^n} converges if (1 − e^{−2Tγ})/γ · C < 1" (Theorem 3.1) and "the convergence rate decreases in the number of players N" (Remark 3.1). — [arXiv 1903.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09376)
- Han and Hu, "Deep Fictitious Play for Finding Markovian Nash Equilibrium in Multi-Agent Games", arXiv 1912.01809, Proceedings of MSML 2020, PMLR 107:221–245. Abstract: "we recast the N-player game into N decoupled decision problems (one for each player) and solve them iteratively. The individual decision problem is characterized by a semilinear Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation, to solve which we employ the recently developed deep BSDE method ... Even for a fifty-player game with the presence of common noise, the proposed algorithm still finds the approximate Nash equilibrium accurately." The abstract page has no mention of partial information, filtering or asymmetric information. — [arXiv 1912.01809](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01809); [PMLR](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v107/han20a/han20a.pdf)
- Han, Hu and Long, "Convergence of Deep Fictitious Play for Stochastic Differential Games", arXiv 2008.05519 (v2, 21 Mar 2021). "with small time duration, we prove that the solutions to the decoupled sub-problems, if solved repeatedly and exactly at each stage, converge to the true Nash equilibrium; ... and that the strategy based on numerical solutions forms an ε-Nash equilibrium". The paper proposes two decoupling options, "I. Fictitious play" and "II. Policy update". The state is "a common process to all players" with Markovian policies α(t, X_t); no partial information appears in the fetched pages. Numerical example: "The final RSE for the Deep BSDE method is 0.27%." — [arXiv 2008.05519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05519)
- Han–Hu–Long cite Casgrain, Ning and Jaimungal, "Deep Q-learning for Nash equilibria: Nash-DQN", arXiv 1904.10554 as a related DNN game solver (not opened; full-information per my memory, unverified). — [arXiv 2008.05519 reference list](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05519)
- Hu's later work on partial information: Ralston, Yang and Hu, "Information Revelation and Alignment Faking in Stochastic Differential Games", arXiv 2603.17197 (v2, 1 Sep 2026). "We study this problem via a two-player linear-quadratic stochastic differential game under partial information, in which each player knows its own coupling parameter and models the opponent's hidden parameter through a prior. Starting from the full-information game, we characterize the Nash equilibrium by coupled Riccati equations. We then define baseline implementable controls by averaging the equilibrium under each player's prior." The authors state: "Because our game features hidden parameters without common priors, and each player's prior is itself unknown to the opponent, we define implementable controls by averaging the full-information Nash equilibrium under each player's prior". Future work includes "incorporating dynamic learning and belief updating". Numerics: Euler–Maruyama with N = 100 steps, "N_MC = 50,000 independent samples", Algorithm 1 gradient ascent converging "within 70 iterations". — [arXiv 2603.17197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17197)
- Zhou, Ralston, Yang and Hu, "Adversarial Decision-Making in Partially Observable Multi-Agent Systems: A Sequential Hypothesis Testing Approach", arXiv 2509.03727: "partially observable Stackelberg game"; "we derive a semi-explicit optimal control solution for the blue team within a linear-quadratic setting" and "develop iterative and machine learning-based methods to characterize the red team's optimal response". — [arXiv 2509.03727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03727)
- Hu's group also has "Stochastic Delay Differential Games: Financial Modeling and Machine Learning Algorithms", arXiv 2307.06450 (closed-loop Nash of delay games; full information per abstract snippet, not opened). — [alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2307.06450)

### Inferences
- The deep fictitious play line solves N decoupled full-information control problems per stage. Replacing each sub-problem by a regression of a first-order-condition target on each player's own observation increments is a different step, not a variant that appears in this literature.
- Hu's open-loop formulation already has the structure "player i's control is a functional of noise paths" but with a single common filtration. The proposed method's distinguishing feature is per-player filtrations.

### Gaps
- I did not open Casgrain–Ning–Jaimungal Nash-DQN (arXiv 1904.10554) or Hu's 2307.06450; both are believed full-information.

## Deep BSDE/FBSDE solvers for games and for partially observed control; regression Monte Carlo (Bender–Denk); fictitious play convergence (Andersson et al.)

### Takeaway
All three identifiers exist and are correctly described. Deng–Su–Zhou (arXiv 2007.11781) is the only deep FBSDE game solver under partial information found; it uses a two-stage scheme (RNN filter learned by least squares, then deep FBSDE) with all agents observing the same prices. Bender–Denk 2007 is a forward Picard-type scheme for BSDEs. Andersson–Andersson–Ljung (arXiv 2607.08861) proves geometric convergence of fictitious play for fully coupled FBSDEs under full information. A 2026 "Deep FBSDE Picard Solver" exists but for a full-information mean-field Stackelberg game.

### Cited Findings
- Deng, Su and Zhou, "Relative wealth concerns with partial information and heterogeneous priors", arXiv 2007.11781 (q-fin.PM, 23 Jul 2020). Abstract: "We establish a Nash equilibrium in a market with N agents with the performance criteria of relative wealth level when the market return is unobservable. Each investor has a random prior belief on the return rate of the risky asset. ... Two sets of deep neural networks are used for the numerical computation to first find each investor's estimate of the mean return rate and then solve the FBSDEs. ... We demonstrate the efficiency and accuracy by a base-case comparison with the solution from the finite difference scheme in the linear case and apply the algorithm to the general case of nonlinear hidden variable process." — [arXiv 2007.11781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11781)
- Information structure in Deng–Su–Zhou: "The observable filtration is the one generated by the stock prices. We denote by F^S_t for the σ-algebra generated by (S_u)_{u≤t}." and "the information heterogeneity is only on the prior estimates. All agents follow the Bayesian learning procedure with unlimited information processing ability." So all agents share one observation filtration; only initial beliefs differ. — [arXiv 2007.11781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11781)
- Numerical scheme in Deng–Su–Zhou: "The numerical scheme is conducted in two stages. In Stage I, we estimate the stock returns using an L2 projection. The recurrent neural network (RNN) is used in order to exploit the time series feature of the input ... In Stage II, we solve the FBSDE, again using neural networks as function approximators. ### Inferences
- Deng–Su–Zhou's Stage I is a least-squares regression of an unobserved quantity on an RNN of the observation path, which is the same statistical operation as the proposed method's regression of Λ^i on F^i. The difference is that they regress the drift (a filter) and then solve a full-information FBSDE on the filtered drift; the proposed method regresses the first-order-condition target directly and never forms a filter.
- The Wu–Jaimungal conclusion is an explicit statement in the literature that a numerical method for Nash FBSDEs with differing filtrations is missing.

- Bender–Denk's own text on Picard iteration was not read (paywall).

## Guan–Shames–Summers arXiv 2608.13755 and 2603.18304

### Takeaway
Both exist and match the description: two-player zero-sum discrete-time LQG games with private noisy measurements, solved by alternating best responses with Kalman-filter belief states (2608.13755) or by a coupled forward-backward recursion with depth-one beliefs (2603.18304). They are discrete-time, zero-sum, two-player, and compute explicit belief states.

### Cited Findings
- Guan, Shames and Summers, "Best Response Dynamics for Zero-Sum Dynamic Games with Partial-Asymmetric Information", arXiv 2608.13755 (math.OC, 13 Aug 2026). Dynamics "x_{t+1} = A x_t + B^(1) u^(1)_t + B^(2) u^(2)_t + w_t" (discrete time, infinite-horizon average cost). Information: "each player observes only their own noisy measurement y^(i)_t and knows their own past controls u^(i)_t, but has no access to the opponent's measurements and controls." — [arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755)
- Method: "player 1's best response for this game is an LQG controller, combining a linear quadratic regulator (LQR) with a Kalman filter ... player 2's best response is an augmented LQG controller in which the Kalman filter estimates not only the underlying system state but also the state estimate of player 1 (a 1st-order belief state). With each successive best response between these two players, they form increasingly higher-order belief states, leading to an infinite regress where their internal state dimension increases towards infinity. However, we observed in extensive numerical experiments that the game's value converges after just a few iterations". — [arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755)
- On common-information methods: "for general games lacking this common information structure, to the best of our knowledge, no known sequential decomposition method exists. ... Our best response dynamics, by contrast, only require solving standard LQG subproblems at each iteration, so they apply directly without assuming any common information structure." — [arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755)
- Validation: spacecraft pursuit-evasion with n = 4, Δt = 0.1 s, and "1000 random open-loop stable systems with higher-dimensional states, inputs, and measurements (n = 4 and m_1 = m_2 = p_1 = p_2 = 2)"; Table 1 shows about 82% of controllability-Gramian eigenvalues below 1e-4. Open-source code at github.com/TSummersLab/partial-asym-info-best-response. The paper is an extension of the authors' reference [34] (earlier best-response paper without feed-through; identifier not shown in the fetched pages). — [arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755)
- Guan, Shames and Summers, "Forward-Backward Dynamic Programming for LQG Dynamic Games with Partial and Asymmetric Information", arXiv 2603.18304 (math.OC, 18 Mar 2026). "We formulate and study a class of two-player zero-sum stochastic dynamic games with partial and asymmetric information. ... we focus on a linear quadratic Gaussian (LQG) model and consider strategies with limited internal state dimension. We present a novel iterative forward-backward algorithm to jointly compute belief states and equilibrium strategies and value functions for a finite-horizon problem." Discrete time ("x_{t+1} = A_t x_t + ..."). — [arXiv 2603.18304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18304)
- Key assumption: "Assumption 2 (Bounded Depth of Reasoning and Self-projecting Beliefs). ... we restrict each player's depth of reasoning to first-order belief ... E[z^j_t | z^i_t] = z^i_t." Remark 1: "Without special information structures such as partially nested and stochastically nested configurations, linear control strategies are not optimal in general for the LQG dynamic games with asymmetric information". Algorithm: "(Σ, F) = Forward(M, K, Γ), (P, K) = Backward(M, F)" iterated to convergence; "There are no existing algorithms to jointly solve these coupled recursions to our best knowledge." Code at gitlab.com/scratch7473433/partialinfodynamicprogramming. — [arXiv 2603.18304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18304)
- Their related-work review (useful map of the control literature): Nayyar, Gupta, Langbort, Başar (IEEE TAC 59(3):555–570, 2013); Gupta, Nayyar, Langbort, Başar (SIAM J. Control Optim. 52(5):3228–3260, 2014); Ouyang, Tavafoghi, Teneketzis (IEEE TAC 62(1):222–237, 2016); Vasal, Sinha, Anastasopoulos (IEEE TAC 64(1):81–96, 2018); Vasal, Anastasopoulos (IEEE TCNS 8(3):1177–1188, 2021); Kartik, Nayyar (Dyn. Games Appl. 11:363–388, 2021); Tang et al. (Dyn. Games Appl. 13:353–411, 2023). Their assessment: "The work discussed above still has a strong focus on approaches that exploit the separation of estimation and control ... none of these works have provided open-source, extensible computational implementations." — [arXiv 2603.18304 reference list](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18304)

### Inferences
- Both papers are restricted to zero-sum, two players, discrete time, and linear strategies with explicit Kalman belief states. The empirical finding that "higher-order belief states provide vanishing benefit" is the kind of claim a method that learns policies on observation histories could test directly in continuous time and for N > 2 nonzero-sum games.

### Gaps
- The earlier best-response paper (their reference [34]) was not identified by arXiv number.

## Multi-agent RL / policy gradient for partially observed continuous-time or finance games; learning methods for Kyle-type models

### Takeaway
Multi-agent deep RL has been applied to partially observed continuous-time dealer games (Cont–Xiong) and to two-agent Almgren–Chriss execution games (Lillo–Macrì; Koulouris–Campajola), always as model-free learning compared against a known Nash benchmark, never as a method whose aim is to compute the partial-information equilibrium itself. For Kyle models, neural-network training reproduces the single-insider single-period equilibrium (Friedrich–Teichmann); no neural or RL solution of a multi-insider Kyle model was found.

Abstract: "We model the interaction of market makers in a dealer market as a stochastic differential game of intensity control with partial information and study the resulting dynamics of bid-ask spreads. Competition among dealers is modeled as a Nash equilibrium, while collusion is described in terms of Pareto optima. Using a decentralized multi-agent deep reinforcement learning algorithm to model how competing market makers learn to adjust their quotes, we show that the interaction of market making algorithms via market prices, without any sharing of information, may give rise to tacit collusion, with spread levels strictly above the competitive equilibrium level." — [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:38c0a628-d6cc-4160-be23-9337a1c02951). The search summary adds that dealers "make decisions based on partial information related to their own inventory, quotes and profits" and that the Nash equilibrium is "characterized in terms of coupled Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations" (secondary, from search snippets; Wiley page returned 403). — [search](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/mafi.12401)

Numerical experiments "across both symmetric and asymmetric interaction networks". Information restriction is on the policy class (own state), not a hidden state. — [arXiv 2602.16555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16555)
- Chu, Gao and Zhang, "Learning under Opponent Unawareness in Linear-Quadratic Stochastic Games", arXiv 2608.08268: players access "a common state and their own action history"; each runs "a single-agent ε-greedy iterated least-squares algorithm"; "players' learning dynamics converge almost surely to the complete-information Nash equilibrium." Whether continuous time is not confirmed from the abstract page. — [arXiv 2608.08268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08268)
- "Certified Nash Equilibrium Learning for Nonzero-Sum Jump-Diffusion Games: Policy Iteration and Off-Policy Q-Learning", arXiv 2611.21272 (16 Nov 2026): "two model-free reinforcement learning methods for infinite-horizon N-player linear-quadratic nonzero-sum stochastic differential games" (snippet only; full information presumed, not opened). — [alphaXiv listing](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.21272)
- ABIDES-MARL (arXiv 2511.02016) frames execution under endogenous liquidity as "a finite-horizon stochastic game" with the open-loop Nash equilibrium as reference (snippet only). — [arXiv 2511.02016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02016)

### Inferences
- The MARL finance papers use the known full-information Nash equilibrium only as a benchmark and report that learning dynamics drift to collusive outcomes. None targets the partial-information Nash equilibrium as the object to compute, and none validates against it.
- No neural or RL method for Kyle models with multiple informed traders (Foster–Viswanathan type, where traders forecast the forecasts of others) was found; Choi–Larsen (2026) is theoretical and Babichenko (below) treats the Kyle–Back embedding analytically.

### Gaps
- The Cont–Xiong full text (observation structure of each dealer, continuous-time details) was not read; only the abstract was verified.
- Lillo–Macrì's and Koulouris–Campajola's exact observation sets were not read beyond the abstracts.

## Decentralized / partially observed LQG games in control engineering: non-classical information, learning-based approaches

### Takeaway
The control literature on asymmetric-information LQG games is discrete-time and computes equilibria through explicit belief recursions (Kalman filters with strategy-dependent covariances, common-information beliefs, or structured PBE). Learning-based methods for non-classical information exist for teams (Witsenhausen) but not for continuous-time nonzero-sum games with two-way private noisy observations.

### Cited Findings
- Vasal and Anastasopoulos, "Signaling equilibria for dynamic LQG games with asymmetric information", arXiv 1606.04960 (cs.GT, 15 Jun 2016; published IEEE TCNS 8(3):1177–1188, 2021 per the Guan et al. reference list). "We consider a finite horizon dynamic game with two players who observe their types privately and take actions, which are publicly observed." Linear strategies plus Gaussian beliefs form a PBE; "a backward-forward algorithmic solution where the backward phase solves matrix equations for each state estimate covariance realization, while the forward phase applies Kalman filter recursions with strategy-dependent covariance matrices." — [arXiv 1606.04960](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04960)
- Ouyang, Tavafoghi and Teneketzis, "Dynamic Games with Asymmetric Information: Common Information Based Perfect Bayesian Equilibria and Sequential Decomposition", arXiv 1510.07001, IEEE TAC 62(1):222–237: "Using the common information among agents, researchers introduce a subclass of PBE called common information based perfect Bayesian equilibria (CIB-PBE), and provide a sequential decomposition" leading to "a backward induction algorithm to compute CIB-PBE" (search summary). — [arXiv 1510.07001](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07001)
- Hambly, Xu and Yang, "Linear-quadratic Gaussian Games with Asymmetric Information: Belief Corrections Using the Opponents Actions", arXiv 2307.15842: "two-player non-zero-sum linear-quadratic Gaussian games ... The system is partially observable with asymmetric information available to the players. In particular, each player has a private and noisy measurement of the state process but can see the history of their opponent's actions." Provides "a closed-form solution for the Bayesian updating rule of their belief process" and "a Nash equilibrium which is linear in the estimation of the state"; application to bargaining. Discrete vs continuous time not stated on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2307.15842](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15842)
- Witsenhausen counterexample numerics: Telsang, Djouadi and Charalambous, "General Decentralized Stochastic Optimal Control via Change of Measure: Applications to the Witsenhausen Counterexample", arXiv 2509.11013: "global and person-by-person (PbP) optimality conditions ... using a discrete-time version of Girsanov's change of measure", numerical solution by Gauss–Hermite quadrature; teams only. — [arXiv 2509.11013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11013). Deterministic annealing (arXiv 1403.5315, 1402.0525, 1607.02893) with "lowest known cost obtained was 0.16692291" (search snippet). — [arXiv 1403.5315](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5315). Neural-network approach "Numerical Solutions to the Witsenhausen Counterexample by Approximating Networks" (Baglietto, Parisini, Zoppoli, IEEE TAC, c. 2001; authors and venue from memory, title confirmed by search result only; unverified). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3024143_Numerical_Solutions_to_the_Witsenhausen_Counterexample_by_Approximating_Networks)
- Chen and Gündüz, "The Control Plant as A Communication Channel: Implicit Communication for Decentralized LQG Control", arXiv 2605.01903: leader–follower LQG with no communication link, "the control plant itself serves as a communication channel"; analytical conditions for the fully actuated case and numerical solution otherwise. Team problem. — [arXiv 2605.01903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01903)
- Sayin et al., "Decentralized Q-Learning in Zero-sum Markov Games", NeurIPS 2021 (discrete, from search listing; not opened). — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/985e9a46e10005356bbaf194249f6856-Paper.pdf)
- Continuous-time LQ games with partial observation but nested or one-sided structure (titles from alphaXiv listing; not opened): "Linear-Quadratic Zero-Sum Stochastic Differential Game with Partial Observation", arXiv 2510.24493; "Linear-Quadratic Non-zero Sum Differential Game with Asymmetric Delayed Information", arXiv 2510.21152; "Noncooperative Game in Multi-controller System under Delayed and Asymmetric Information", arXiv 2603.29783; Shi, Wang, Xiong, "Leader-Follower Stochastic Differential Game with Asymmetric Information and Applications", arXiv 1509.03982 (follower information "based on some sub-σ-algebra"). These are maximum-principle characterizations, not numerical methods. — [alphaXiv listings](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.24493)

### Inferences
- Guan–Shames–Summers' own summary of this literature ("none of these works have provided open-source, extensible computational implementations") and their statement that no sequential decomposition exists without common information indicate that numerical methods for general non-nested LQG games are new even in discrete time.
- Learning-based (neural) numerics for non-classical information are documented only for the two-stage Witsenhausen team problem.

### Gaps
- Hambly–Xu–Yang's time setting and any numerical algorithm were not read.
- No continuous-time learning method for non-classical information games was found; this may be absence from the literature rather than a search miss, but the search did not cover IEEE TAC/Automatica directly.

## Games where beliefs-about-beliefs do not close (Townsend 1983): numerical solutions, especially policies learned directly on observation histories

### Takeaway
One 2026 paper (Babichenko, arXiv 2603.12140) claims the first exact equilibrium characterization of finite-player continuous-time LQ games with endogenous private signals and computes it by Picard iteration on a deterministic impulse-response system, with no truncation of the hierarchy. It is restricted to linear-Gaussian noise-state strategies and computes the filter kernels explicitly. No paper was found that learns policies directly on observation histories to bypass the hierarchy.

### Cited Findings
- Babichenko, "Forecasting and Manipulating the Forecasts of Others", arXiv 2603.12140 (v3, math.OC, 23 Mar 2026; UC Santa Barbara, advisor Tomoyuki Ichiba). Abstract: "When actions reshape opponents' signals, each agent's optimal response depends on an infinite hierarchy of beliefs about beliefs (Townsend, 1983) that has resisted exact analysis for four decades. We provide the first exact equilibrium characterization of finite-player continuous-time linear–quadratic games with endogenous signals. Estimating primitive shocks rather than the state collapses the belief hierarchy onto deterministic impulse-response maps, reducing Nash equilibrium to a deterministic fixed point with no truncation and no large-population limit." — [arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Model: "dX_t = (A(t)X_t + Σ_i D^i_t) dt + Σ(t) dW^0_t", observations "dY^i_t = Γ^i(t) X_t dt + E^i dW_t" with independent noises, "Player i's information at time t is their observation history, F^i_t := σ(Y^i_s : s ≤ t)." Two-player example: "dY^1_t = √p X_t dt + dW^1_t, dY^2_t = √p X_t dt + dW^2_t" with opposing targets ±1. Non-nested private signals. — [arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Computation: "Equilibrium is computed by iterating the best-response operator on the deterministic impulse-response system (Section 4), discretized on a uniform grid of N = 40 points; no Monte Carlo simulation is needed. Parameters: T = 1, x_0 = 0, r = 0.1, no terminal cost. The Picard residual reaches 10^−5 in 19 iterations (≈ 10 ms in C++), decaying geometrically." Code: github.com/sbabichenko/Noise-State-Games. — [arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Structure of the fixed point: strategies are "noise-state linear", D^i_t = D̄^i_t + ∫_0^t D^i_t(u) d_u Ŵ^i_t(u) with Ŵ^i_t(u) := E[W_u | F^i_t]; the costate carries an "information wedge" V^i_t that "prices the marginal value of shifting opponents' posteriors; it vanishes precisely when signals are exogenous." Remark 4.1: "Discretizing time, each observation increment is affine in the noise increments ΔW_{1:k}, so by joint Gaussianity the conditional mean E[ΔW_{1:k} | ΔY^i_{1:k}] is a deterministic linear projection; each layer of 'beliefs about beliefs' is a further deterministic composition of projections, which is why the regress closes." — [arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Open question stated by the author: "The main open question is whether the deterministic impulse-response class is without loss of generality." Related work cited: Nimark (2017) truncation of the hierarchy as "the closest existing method in spirit"; Huo and Takayama (2023) on exogenous-signal frequency-domain methods that "cannot admit a finite-state representation in general" with endogenous information; Foster and Viswanathan (J. Finance 51(4):1437–1478, 1996); Kyle–Back embedding "extends to multi-trader settings where guess-and-verify breaks down." — [arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Welfare result: "nearly all welfare gains from pooling information come from eliminating bilateral belief manipulation rather than from improving state estimation" (gains "roughly 0.02–0.05 under cooperation versus 0.3–0.5 under competition"). — [arXiv 2603.12140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12140)
- Guan–Shames–Summers (above) handle the hierarchy numerically by growing Kalman filters and observe convergence of the value within a few iterations; Guan et al. (2603.18304) truncate at first-order beliefs by assumption. — [arXiv 2608.13755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13755), [arXiv 2603.18304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18304)
- Ralston–Yang–Hu (2603.17197) cite Townsend explicitly and avoid the hierarchy by prior averaging rather than solving it. — [arXiv 2603.17197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17197)

### Inferences
- Babichenko's Remark 4.1 is a theoretical statement of why a regression of a Gaussian target on each player's own observation increments is exact in the LQ-Gaussian case; the proposed deep Picard regression can be read as a learned, Monte Carlo version of his deterministic Picard iteration that does not assume the noise-state linear class and does not form the filter kernels. A direct comparison on his two-player tracking example (T = 1, r = 0.1, p ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 10}) would be a natural validation.
- No work was found that learns observation-history policies to sidestep the belief hierarchy in continuous-time games. The closest are the discrete-time MARL finance papers, which do learn on histories but do not target or validate the partial-information equilibrium.

### Gaps
- Nimark (2017) and Huo–Takayama (2023) are working papers and were not opened.
- "Learning, Misspecification, and Cognitive Arbitrage in Linear-Quadratic Network Games", arXiv 2603.17157 (agents "observe noisy aggregate signals") appeared in search but was not opened.
